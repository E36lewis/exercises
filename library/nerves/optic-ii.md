The optic nerve (II) is a cranial nerve that carries visual information from the retina to the brain. It is the second cranial nerve, 
and it is responsible for transmitting visual information from the retina to the brain.

The optic nerve is a paired nerve, meaning that there is one optic nerve for each eye. The optic nerves are attached to the retina at the optic disc, 
which is a small, blind spot in the retina. The optic nerves then travel through the optic canal in the skull and meet at the optic chiasm, where they 
cross over. After the optic nerves cross over, they continue on as the optic tracts. The optic tracts then project to the lateral geniculate nucleus of 
the thalamus, which is a relay station for visual information. From the lateral geniculate nucleus, the visual information is projected to the primary 
visual cortex, which is located in the occipital lobe of the brain.
