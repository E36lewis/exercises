The maxillary nerve is the second of the three branches of the trigeminal nerve. It is a sensory nerve that supplies sensation to the midface, 
including the upper jaw, the skin of the cheek, the nasal cavity, the sinuses, the palate, and the teeth. It also carries parasympathetic fibers to the lacrimal 
gland, the salivary glands, and the mucous membranes of the nose and sinuses.

The maxillary nerve arises from the trigeminal ganglion, which is located in the middle cranial fossa. It then passes through the foramen rotundum and enters 
the pterygopalatine fossa. In the pterygopalatine fossa, the maxillary nerve gives off several branches, including:

The zygomatic nerve, which supplies sensation to the skin of the cheek. 
The infraorbital nerve, which supplies sensation to the skin of the upper lip, the side of the nose, and the upper jaw. 
The superior alveolar nerves, which supply sensation to the teeth of the upper jaw. 
The middle meningeal nerve, which supplies blood to the meninges (the membranes that surround the brain). 
The pterygopalatine ganglion, which relays parasympathetic fibers to the lacrimal gland, the salivary glands, and the mucous membranes of the nose and sinuses.
