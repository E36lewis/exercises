The saphenous nerve is a sensory nerve that arises from the femoral nerve in the thigh. It descends through the 
adductor canal and pierces the deep fascia of the leg, becoming subcutaneous. It then passes down the medial side of the leg, 
supplying sensation to the skin of the medial thigh, knee, leg, and foot. It terminates at the medial malleolus of the ankle, 
where it joins the sural nerve to form the medial calcaneal nerve.

The saphenous nerve is the largest terminal branch of the femoral nerve. It is responsible for providing sensory innervation to 
the medial thigh, knee, leg, and foot.
