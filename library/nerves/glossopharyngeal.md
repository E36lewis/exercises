The glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX) is a mixed cranial nerve, meaning it carries both motor and sensory information. It arises from the medulla oblongata and exits the skull through the jugular foramen.

The glossopharyngeal nerve has four major functions:

Swallowing: The glossopharyngeal nerve innervates the stylopharyngeal muscle, which helps to raise the pharynx during swallowing.
Taste: The glossopharyngeal nerve carries taste information from the posterior one-third of the tongue.
Secretion: The glossopharyngeal nerve innervates the parotid salivary gland.
Vasomotor control: The glossopharyngeal nerve helps to regulate blood flow to the carotid arteries.

The glossopharyngeal nerve is susceptible to injury from trauma, tumors, and infections. Symptoms of glossopharyngeal nerve injury can include:

Difficulty swallowing, 
Pain in the throat, 
Numbness or tingling in the back of the tongue, 
Dry mouth, and
Irregular heartbeat
