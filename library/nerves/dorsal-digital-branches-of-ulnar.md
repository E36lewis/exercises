The dorsal digital branches of the ulnar nerve are peripheral nerve branches that originate from the ulnar nerve in the hand. 
These branches provide 
sensory innervation to the dorsal (back) aspect of the fingers and play a crucial role in transmitting sensory information from the 
fingers to the central nervous system.

Dorsal Digital Branches:
The dorsal digital branches arise from the ulnar nerve as it enters the hand.
These branches typically originate in the region of the Guyon's canal, which is a narrow passageway formed by the pisiform and hamate bones in the wrist.
The dorsal digital branches extend along the dorsal aspect of the fingers, primarily on the ulnar side (little finger side) of the hand.
Each branch supplies sensory innervation to the skin on the dorsal surface of its respective finger.
The branches usually divide further into smaller terminal branches that reach the fingertips.
The dorsal digital branches of the ulnar nerve are responsible for transmitting sensory information from the dorsal skin of the fingers. 
They play a vital role in the perception of touch, temperature, pressure, and other sensory stimuli from the dorsal surfaces of the fingers.
