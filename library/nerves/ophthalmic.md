The ophthalmic nerve (V1) is the first of the three divisions of the trigeminal nerve (CN V). It is purely sensory and supplies 
the skin of the forehead, upper eyelid, eyeball, lacrimal gland, ethmoidal sinuses, and dura mater of the anterior cranial fossa.

The ophthalmic nerve arises from the trigeminal ganglion, which is located in the middle cranial fossa. The nerve passes through the superior 
orbital fissure and enters the orbit. It then divides into five branches:

Lacrimal nerve: This nerve supplies the lacrimal gland and the skin of the upper eyelid.
Frontal nerve: This nerve supplies the skin of the forehead and scalp.
Nasociliary nerve: This nerve supplies the nasal cavity, the medial canthus of the eye, and the skin of the upper eyelid.
Supraorbital nerve: This nerve supplies the skin of the forehead and scalp.
Anterior ethmoidal nerve: This nerve supplies the ethmoidal sinuses and the skin of the medial canthus of the eye.
