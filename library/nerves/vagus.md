The vagus nerve, also known as the tenth cranial nerve, is the longest of the cranial nerves. It is a mixed nerve, meaning that 
it carries both motor and sensory fibers. The vagus nerve is responsible for a variety of functions, including:

Innervation of the heart, lungs, and digestive tract. The vagus nerve sends parasympathetic fibers to the heart, lungs, and digestive tract. 
These fibers slow down the heart rate, relax the airways, and increase digestive secretions. 
Control of the vocal cords. The vagus nerve innervates the vocal cords. This allows us to produce a variety of sounds, including speech. 
Taste sensation. The vagus nerve carries taste sensation from the back of the tongue. 
Reflexes. The vagus nerve is involved in a number of reflexes, including coughing, sneezing, and vomiting. 
The vagus nerve is a very important nerve. It is involved in a variety of functions, including heart rate, breathing, digestion, and speech.
