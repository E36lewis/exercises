The trochlear nerve is the fourth of twelve paired cranial nerves in humans. It innervates the superior oblique muscle of the eye, 
which allows the eye to move downward and inward. The trochlear nerve is the smallest and thinnest of the cranial nerves. It is also the only 
cranial nerve that emerges from the dorsal surface of the brainstem.

The trochlear nerve arises from the dorsal midbrain, just below the inferior colliculus. It then courses posterolaterally around the brainstem 
and exits through the superior orbital fissure. The trochlear nerve then passes through the trochlea, a small fibrous loop that is attached to the 
frontal bone. The trochlear nerve then inserts into the superior oblique muscle.

The trochlear nerve is responsible for innervating the superior oblique muscle of the eye. This muscle is responsible for allowing the eye to move 
downward and inward. The trochlear nerve is also responsible for maintaining the alignment of the eyes.

Damage to the trochlear nerve can cause double vision, difficulty with downward gaze, and difficulty with convergence. Treatment for trochlear 
nerve damage may include surgery or physical therapy.
