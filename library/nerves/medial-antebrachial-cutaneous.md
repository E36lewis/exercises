The medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (MACN) is a sensory nerve that arises from the medial cord of the brachial plexus. It supplies sensation 
to the skin of the medial forearm.

The MACN arises from the medial cord of the brachial plexus, and pierces the deep fascia of the arm about 10 cm proximal to the medial epicondyle of the 
humerus. It then divides into two branches:

The anterior branch supplies sensation to the skin of the medial side of the forearm, as far distally as the wrist joint.
The posterior branch supplies sensation to the skin of the medial side of the forearm, as far distally as the olecranon process of the ulna.
