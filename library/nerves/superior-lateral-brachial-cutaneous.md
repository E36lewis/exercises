The superior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve (SLCBN), also known as the upper lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm, is a sensory nerve 
that arises from the axillary nerve. It supplies sensation to the skin over the lower two-thirds of the posterior part of the deltoid muscle, 
as well as that covering the long head of the triceps brachii.

The SLCBN arises from the axillary nerve, which is a branch of the brachial plexus. The axillary nerve arises from the posterior cord of the 
brachial plexus. The SLCBN pierces the deep fascia of the arm and divides into two branches:

The medial branch supplies sensation to the skin over the lower two-thirds of the posterior part of the deltoid muscle. 
The lateral branch supplies sensation to the skin over the long head of the triceps brachii. 
Injury to the SLCBN can cause numbness and tingling in the area that it supplies. In some cases, it can also cause weakness in the 
muscles of the deltoid and triceps brachii.
