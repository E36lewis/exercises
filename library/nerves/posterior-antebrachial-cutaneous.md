The posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (PACN) is a sensory nerve that supplies the skin on the back of the forearm. It is a 
branch of the radial nerve. The PACN arises from the radial nerve in the spiral groove of the humerus, just above the elbow. It then travels 
down the back of the forearm, superficial to the triceps brachii muscle. The PACN divides into two branches, the medial and lateral branches. 
The medial branch supplies sensation to the skin on the medial side of the forearm, and the lateral branch supplies sensation to the skin on the 
lateral side of the forearm.

Damage to the PACN can cause numbness and tingling in the skin on the back of the forearm. It can also make it difficult to feel objects that are held in the hand.
