The superficial branch of the radial nerve is a sensory nerve that arises from the radial nerve in the forearm. 
It supplies sensation to the skin on the back of the hand and the dorsum of the thumb, index finger, and middle finger. It also 
supplies articular branches to the wrist and elbow joints.

The superficial branch of the radial nerve arises from the radial nerve in the spiral groove of the humerus, just above the elbow. 
It then travels down the back of the forearm, superficial to the triceps brachii muscle. The superficial branch of the radial nerve 
divides into two branches, the medial and lateral branches. The medial branch supplies sensation to the skin on the medial side of the 
back of the hand, and the lateral branch supplies sensation to the skin on the lateral side of the back of the hand.

Damage to the superficial branch of the radial nerve can cause numbness and tingling in the skin on the back of the hand and the dorsum 
of the thumb, index finger, and middle finger. It can also make it difficult to feel objects that are held in the hand.
