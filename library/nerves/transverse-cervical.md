The transverse cervical nerve (superficial cervical or cutaneous cervical), turns around the posterior border of the sternocleidomastoideus about its 
middle, and, passing obliquely forward beneath the external jugular vein to the anterior border of the muscle, 
it perforates the deep cervical fascia, and divides beneath the Platysma into ascending and descending branches, 
which are distributed to the antero-lateral parts of the neck. It provides cutaneous innervation to this area.

During dissection, the sternocleidomastoid (SCM) is the landmark. The transverse cervical nerves will pass 
horizontally directly over the SCM from Erb's point.
