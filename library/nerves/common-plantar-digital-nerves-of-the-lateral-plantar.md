The lateral plantar nerve is a branch of the tibial nerve, which is one of the major nerves in the lower limb.
The lateral plantar nerve runs along the lateral (outer) side of the foot, supplying various structures in that region.
As the lateral plantar nerve travels through the foot, it gives rise to multiple branches known as the common plantar digital nerves.

Sensory Innervation:

The common plantar digital nerves of the lateral plantar nerve provide sensory innervation to specific areas of the foot.
These branches supply sensation to the plantar (sole) surface of the toes, including the pads of the toes on the lateral side.
The common plantar digital nerves transmit sensory information such as touch, pressure, temperature, 
and pain from the plantar surface of the toes to the central nervous system.

Function:

The common plantar digital nerves play a vital role in providing sensory feedback from the plantar surface of the toes on the lateral side.
They contribute to the overall sensory perception of the foot, assisting in balance, coordination, and protective responses.
Additionally, they play a role in proprioception, providing information about the position and movement of the toes during weight-bearing activities.
