The infrapatellar branch, also known as the infrapatellar nerve or patellar branch, is a sensory nerve that arises from 
the tibial nerve. It provides innervation to the skin on the front of the knee, specifically in the region just below the patella (kneecap).

Origin and Course:

The infrapatellar branch originates from the tibial nerve, which is a major branch of the sciatic nerve in the leg.
It typically arises at or below the level of the popliteal fossa (the hollow space at the back of the knee).
The infrapatellar branch descends anteriorly (towards the front) along the leg, reaching the infrapatellar region located just below the patella.
Innervation and Function:

The infrapatellar branch provides sensory innervation to the skin on the anterior (front) surface of the knee, specifically in the infrapatellar area.
This area includes the skin directly below the patella, extending down towards the tibial tuberosity.
The nerve is responsible for transmitting sensory information from the skin in its distribution area, allowing for the perception of touch, temperature, pressure, and other sensory stimuli on the front of the knee.
The infrapatellar branch is an important nerve for the sensory innervation of the knee region. It plays a role in transmitting sensory information from the skin 
on the front of the knee, providing feedback about touch, pain, and temperature. It helps to maintain normal sensory function in this area.
