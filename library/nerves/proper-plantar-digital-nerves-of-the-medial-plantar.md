The medial plantar nerve is a branch of the tibial nerve, which is one of the major nerves in the lower limb.
The medial plantar nerve courses along the medial (inner) side of the foot, supplying various structures in that region.
As the medial plantar nerve travels through the foot, it gives rise to multiple branches known as the proper plantar digital nerves.

Sensory Innervation:

The proper plantar digital nerves of the medial plantar nerve provide sensory innervation to specific areas of the foot.
These branches supply sensation to the plantar (sole) surface of the toes, including the pads of the toes.
The proper plantar digital nerves transmit sensory information such as touch, pressure, temperature, and pain from the plantar surface of the toes to the central nervous system.

Function:

The proper plantar digital nerves play a vital role in providing sensory feedback from the plantar surface of the toes.
They contribute to the overall sensory perception of the foot, assisting in balance, coordination, and protective responses.
Additionally, they play a role in proprioception, providing information about the position and movement of the toes during weight-bearing activities.
It's important to note that the exact branching pattern and distribution of the proper plantar digital nerves of the medial plantar nerve can vary among individuals.
