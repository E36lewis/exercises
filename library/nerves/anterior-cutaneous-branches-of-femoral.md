The anterior cutaneous branches of the femoral nerve are two nerves that arise from the femoral nerve in the femoral triangle. They supply sensation to the skin of the anterior and medial thigh.

The medial femoral cutaneous nerve of the thigh arises from the femoral nerve in the femoral triangle, and pierces the fascia lata about 1 cm below the inguinal ligament. It then descends along the medial side 
of the thigh, and supplies sensation to the skin of the medial thigh as far as the knee.

The intermediate femoral cutaneous nerve of the thigh arises from the femoral nerve in the femoral triangle, and pierces the fascia lata about 7.5 cm below the inguinal ligament. 
It then divides into two branches, which supply sensation to the skin of the anteromedial thigh.

Damage to the anterior cutaneous branches of the femoral nerve can cause numbness and tingling in the skin of the anterior and medial thigh. This can make it difficult to feel 
objects in the affected area, and can also make it difficult to walk and run.
