The muscular branches of the deep fibular nerve, also known as the anterior tibial nerve, provide motor innervation to several 
muscles in the anterior compartment of the leg. These branches play a crucial role in controlling movements of the foot and ankle. 

Origin and Course:

The deep fibular nerve arises from the common fibular nerve in the upper part of the leg.
It descends along the interosseous membrane, which is a fibrous tissue connecting the tibia and fibula bones in the lower leg.
The deep fibular nerve enters the anterior compartment of the leg and continues its course towards the foot.

Muscular Innervation:

The deep fibular nerve gives off motor branches to the following muscles:

a. Tibialis anterior:

The deep fibular nerve supplies the tibialis anterior muscle, which is located in the front of the leg.
The tibialis anterior muscle is responsible for dorsiflexion of the foot (lifting the foot upwards) and inversion of the foot 
(turning the sole of the foot inward).

b. Extensor digitorum longus:

The deep fibular nerve innervates the extensor digitorum longus muscle, which is situated on the anterior surface of the leg.
The extensor digitorum longus muscle helps with extending the toes and dorsiflexing the foot.

c. Extensor hallucis longus:

The deep fibular nerve provides motor innervation to the extensor hallucis longus muscle, located alongside the other muscles 
in the anterior compartment of the leg.
The extensor hallucis longus muscle is responsible for extending the big toe and assisting in dorsiflexion of the foot.

d. Fibularis tertius (Peroneus tertius):

In some individuals, the deep fibular nerve also supplies the fibularis tertius muscle, which is situated on the lateral side of the leg.
The fibularis tertius muscle assists in dorsiflexion and eversion of the foot.

Function:

The muscular branches of the deep fibular nerve play a vital role in controlling movements involved in dorsiflexion of the foot, 
extension of the toes, and inversion of the foot.
These muscles work together to maintain balance, stabilize the ankle joint, and provide control during activities such as walking, 
running, and standing.
In summary, the muscular branches of the deep fibular nerve innervate the tibialis anterior, extensor digitorum longus, 
extensor hallucis longus, and potentially the fibularis tertius muscles. These motor branches are responsible for controlling various 
movements of the foot and ankle, contributing to overall lower limb function.
