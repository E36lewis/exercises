The sympathetic trunk is a paired bundle of nerve fibers that run from the base of the skull to the coccyx. It is located on 
either side of the vertebral column, just lateral to the vertebral bodies. The sympathetic trunk is made up of a series of ganglia, which are 
collections of nerve cell bodies. The ganglia are connected to each other by nerve fibers.

The sympathetic trunk is part of the autonomic nervous system, which is responsible for involuntary functions such as heart rate, blood 
pressure, and digestion. The sympathetic trunk sends nerve fibers to the heart, lungs, stomach, intestines, and other organs. These nerve 
fibers control the function of these organs.

The sympathetic trunk is also involved in the fight-or-flight response. When a person is faced with a stressful situation, the sympathetic 
trunk sends signals to the adrenal glands, which release hormones that prepare the body for action. These hormones increase heart rate, 
blood pressure, and breathing rate. They also dilate the pupils and constrict the blood vessels in the skin.
