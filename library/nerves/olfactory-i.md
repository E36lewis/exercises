The olfactory nerve (CN I) is the first cranial nerve. It is a sensory nerve that carries information about smell from the nasal 
cavity to the brain. The olfactory nerve is the shortest of the cranial nerves, and it is the only cranial nerve that does not synapse in the thalamus.

The olfactory nerve is responsible for the sense of smell. The olfactory receptors are located in the olfactory epithelium, which is a thin layer of 
tissue that lines the roof of the nasal cavity. The olfactory receptors are specialized neurons that send signals to the olfactory bulb, which is a 
part of the brain that is responsible for processing smell information.

The olfactory nerve is also involved in the emotional and cognitive aspects of smell. For example, the smell of food can make you hungry, and the 
smell of a loved one can make you feel happy.
