The obturator nerve in human anatomy arises from the ventral divisions of the second, third, and fourth lumbar nerves in the 
lumbar plexus; the branch from the third is the largest, while that from the second is often very small. The obturator nerve is responsible for the sensory innervation of the skin of the medial aspect of the thigh.

The nerve is also responsible for the motor innervation of the adductor muscles of the lower limb (external obturator, adductor longus, 
adductor brevis, adductor magnus, gracilis) and the pectineus (inconstant). 
It is, notably, not responsible for the innervation of the obturator internus, despite the similarity in, name.
