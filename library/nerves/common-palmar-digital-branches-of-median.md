The common palmar digital branches of the median nerve are two nerves that arise from the median nerve in the palm of the hand. They run towards the cleft 
between the index and middle fingers, and the middle and ring fingers. Each common palmar digital nerve divides into two proper digital nerves for the adjoining sides of these digits.

The common palmar digital branches of the median nerve provide sensation to the skin on the sides of the index, middle, and ring fingers. They also supply motor 
innervation to the lumbrical muscles, which are small muscles that help to flex the fingers.

Damage to the common palmar digital branches of the median nerve can cause numbness and tingling in the skin on the sides of the index, middle, and ring fingers.
It can also cause weakness in the lumbrical muscles, which can make it difficult to flex the fingers.
