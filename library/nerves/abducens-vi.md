The Abducens nerve, also known as cranial nerve VI or the sixth cranial nerve, is one of the twelve cranial nerves in the human body.
It is responsible for controlling the movement of the lateral rectus muscle, which is one of the six extraocular muscles that control eye movements.

The abducens nerve originates in the brainstem at the level of the pons and exits the skull through the superior orbital fissure, a bony opening located 
in the sphenoid bone. The nerve then travels to the lateral rectus muscle of the eye, which it innervates, causing the eye to move laterally away from the midline.

Damage to the abducens nerve can result in a condition known as sixth nerve palsy, which can cause difficulty in moving the affected eye laterally. 
Symptoms of this condition may include double vision, or diplopia, and difficulty in looking in a specific direction. Causes of sixth nerve palsy can include trauma, 
tumors, inflammation, or other neurological disorders.
