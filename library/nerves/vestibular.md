The vestibular nerve is one of the two branches of the vestibulocochlear nerve (the cochlear nerve being the other). 
It transmits sensory information transmitted by vestibular hair cells located in the two otolith organs (the utricle and the saccule) 
and the three semicircular canals via the vestibular ganglion of Scarpa. Information from the otolith organs reflects gravity and linear 
accelerations of the head. Information from the semicircular canals reflects rotational movement of the head. Both are necessary for the 
sensation of body position and gaze stability in relation to a moving environment.

The vestibular nerve has the following functions:

Maintaining balance. The vestibular nerve sends information to the brain about the position of the head and body in space. This information is 
used to maintain balance and coordination.
Eye movements. The vestibular nerve sends information to the brain about the movement of the head. This information is used to coordinate eye 
movements so that they remain stable even when the head is moving.
Nausea and vomiting. The vestibular nerve is involved in the vomiting reflex. When the vestibular nerve is stimulated, it can cause nausea and vomiting.
