Inferior Lateral Brachial Cutaneous Nerve:
This nerve arises from the axillary nerve or the posterior cord of the brachial plexus.
It travels along the lateral aspect of the upper arm, running parallel to the main course of the axillary nerve.
The inferior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve primarily supplies sensory innervation to the skin on the lower part of the 
lateral aspect of the upper arm.
It may provide sensory branches to the skin around the elbow region, but the extent of its distribution can vary among individuals.
The inferior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve is responsible for transmitting sensory information from the skin in its innervation 
territory. It helps in perceiving touch, temperature, pain, and other sensory stimuli from the lower lateral aspect of the upper arm.
