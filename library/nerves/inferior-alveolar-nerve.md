The inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) is a branch of the mandibular nerve, which is itself the third branch of the trigeminal 
nerve. The IAN is responsible for sensation to the lower teeth and the chin. It also provides motor innervation to the mylohyoid and 
anterior belly of the digastric muscles.

The IAN originates from the mandibular nerve within the infratemporal fossa. It then passes through the mandibular foramen and enters 
the mandibular canal. The IAN travels within the mandibular canal, giving off branches to the molars, premolars, and canines. It also gives 
off a branch called the mylohyoid nerve, which supplies sensation to the mylohyoid and anterior belly of the digastric muscles.

The IAN exits the mandibular canal at the mental foramen. It then divides into two branches: the incisive branch and the mental branch. 
The incisive branch supplies sensation to the incisors and canines. The mental branch supplies sensation to the chin and lower lip.
