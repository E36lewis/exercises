The battle rope wave is rapid up-and-down arm movement that sends waves along a heavy rope anchored at its middle. The shoulders and arms work continually, and the legs and trunk hold you in an athletic stance.

### How to do it
1. Anchor a battle rope around a post or on a stable anchor, with equal lengths on each side.
2. Stand facing the anchor with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent and hips back slightly.
3. Hold one end in each hand with an overhand or neutral grip.
4. Raise and lower your arms alternately in quick waves. Keep your elbows slightly bent.
5. Keep the waves going to the anchor, and keep your trunk braced.
6. Breathe steadily and stop when your waves lose height.

**Tips:** A usual plan is 8 to 10 rounds of 20 seconds of waves and 40 seconds of rest, or 30 seconds on and 30 off. Move from your shoulders, not your back. Avoid anchoring to anything that can come loose.

### In daily life
Shaking out a heavy blanket, raking and shovelling all need the arms to work continuously at shoulder height.

### Why it matters
Combat, water, gymnastics and rock-climbing athletes need shoulders that can keep working for a long time. Rope waves train that endurance, with no impact.
