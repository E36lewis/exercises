This combines a push-up with a row. You do a push-up with your hands on two kettlebells, then row one bell up toward your ribs. It works the chest, back, arms and core, and the anti-twist demand on your trunk is high.

### How to do it
1. Place two kettlebells on the floor about shoulder-width apart and grip the handles in a push-up position, feet wider than hip-width for balance.
2. Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
3. Lower your chest toward the floor by bending your elbows.
4. Push back up to the start.
5. Shift your weight slightly to one side and row the other bell up toward your ribs, keeping your hips level.
6. Lower the bell with control, do another push-up, and row on the other side.

**Tips:** Do not let your hips twist when you row. Keep the supporting kettlebell handle flat on the floor. Use heavy, stable bells, and widen your feet if you wobble. Drop to your knees for the push-up if you need to.

### In daily life
It combines the push you use to get up from the floor with the pull of dragging a heavy bag or opening a stiff door while staying braced.

### Why it matters
Pushing and pulling in one set can help build upper-body strength while the plank position trains your trunk to stay rigid, which supports your spine.
