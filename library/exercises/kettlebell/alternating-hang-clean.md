The alternating hang clean is a kettlebell lift that starts with the bell hanging near your thighs and ends with it resting at your shoulder, one arm at a time. It is mostly a hip movement that works the glutes, hamstrings and thighs, with the back, shoulders and grip joining in.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, holding a kettlebell in one hand in front of your thighs.
2. Push your hips back and let the bell swing between your knees, keeping your back flat.
3. Drive your hips forward and stand tall, pulling the bell up close to your body.
4. As the bell rises, bring your elbow in and let the bell rotate around your wrist to rest against your forearm at shoulder height.
5. Lower it along the same path back to the hang position.
6. Switch hands and repeat.

**Tips:** The power comes from your hips, not from curling your arm. Keep the bell close to your body so it does not crash onto your forearm. Start with a light weight and learn the hip hinge first.

### In daily life
It copies lifting a shopping bag, a toddler or a bag of potting soil from low to chest height without rounding your back.

### Why it matters
It trains the hip snap that powers jumping and sprinting, and builds grip. Doing it one arm at a time can also help your trunk learn to stay level under an uneven load.
