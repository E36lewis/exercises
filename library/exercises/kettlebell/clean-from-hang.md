The clean from hang is a kettlebell lift that brings the bell from the hang position, just in front of your thighs, up to the rack at your shoulder in one fast pull. The power comes from your hips and thighs (glutes, hamstrings, quadriceps), with the upper back and shoulders guiding the bell and the arm finishing the catch.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet about hip-width apart, holding the kettlebell in one hand with your arm hanging in front of your thighs.
2. Push your hips back and let the bell swing back between your legs, keeping your back flat and chest up.
3. Drive your hips forward and stand up fast, keeping the bell close to your body.
4. As the bell rises, pull your elbow back and up, then turn your hand around the handle.
5. Catch the bell in the rack position: elbow tucked against your ribs, forearm upright, fist near your collarbone.
6. Stand tall, then let the bell drop back to the hang under control. Repeat, then switch hands.

**Tips:** Let the hips do the work, not the arm. Keep the bell close so it doesn't slam into your forearm. Choose a weight you can control, and stop if your back rounds.

### In daily life
It copies the hip-driven pull you use to bring a shopping bag or a toddler from low down to chest height.

### Why it matters
It can help build hip power and teach your hands and shoulders to catch a moving load. It is also a good lead-in to the kettlebell press and the snatch.
