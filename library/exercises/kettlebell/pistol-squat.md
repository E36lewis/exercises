A single-leg squat holding a kettlebell, with the other leg held out in front. It works the thighs, glutes and hips, and challenges balance and ankle and hip mobility, so it is an advanced exercise.

### How to do it
1. Stand on one leg, holding the kettlebell at your chest.
2. Extend the other leg in front of you.
3. Brace your abdomen.
4. Squat down on your standing leg, keeping your heel on the floor.
5. Lower as far as you can control.
6. Press through your foot to stand back up.

**Tips:** Keep your knee over your toes and your chest up. Hold something for support at first, or squat to a box. Stop if your knee hurts.

### In daily life
It matches lowering yourself to the ground and getting back up using one leg, such as when one foot is carrying a child or a bag.

### Why it matters
Squatting on one leg can build leg strength and balance, and it shows differences between your sides.
