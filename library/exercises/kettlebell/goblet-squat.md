The kettlebell goblet squat holds a kettlebell against your chest like a cup. The weight in front of you acts as a counterbalance, which makes it easy to sit deep with your chest up while your thighs and glutes do the work.

### How to do it
1. Hold the kettlebell upside down by the horns (the two sides of the handle) with both hands, close to your chest.
2. Stand with your feet a little wider than your shoulders and your toes turned out slightly.
3. Brace your abdomen and keep your elbows tucked in.
4. Bend your hips and knees and sit down between your legs.
5. Lower until your thighs are at least parallel to the floor, if your hips allow.
6. Keep your chest tall and your heels on the floor.
7. Press through your feet to stand back up.

**Tips:** Let your elbows move to the inside of your knees at the bottom. Keep your heels down. Don't let your lower back round at the bottom.

### In daily life
Picking up a heavy bag of groceries or a toddler from the floor looks a lot like this: load held at the chest and a deep, upright sit.

### Why it matters
It teaches the squat pattern with a load that helps you stay upright, which can help beginners learn depth and posture. It also builds the thigh and glute strength used in everyday sitting and standing.
