The kettlebell swing swings a kettlebell from between your legs to chest height by snapping your hips forward. It is a hip hinge, not a squat, so the glutes and hamstrings drive the bell while your arms mostly guide it.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet a little wider than your shoulders and the kettlebell on the floor about a foot in front of you.
2. Hinge at your hips, push your hips back with a flat back and grip the handle with both hands.
3. Hike the bell back between your legs like a football snap.
4. Drive your hips forward powerfully and stand tall.
5. Let the bell float up to about chest height, with your arms relaxed.
6. As it falls, hinge again and let it swing back between your legs.

**Tips:** Don't squat it up, it's a hinge. Keep your back flat. Let your hips do the work and keep your arms loose.

### In daily life
Swinging a bag onto a shelf or throwing a heavy object forward uses the same hip snap and a stable trunk.

### Why it matters
The movement trains the glutes and hamstrings to produce force quickly, which can help build hip power. It also raises your heart rate and tests your grip and trunk.
