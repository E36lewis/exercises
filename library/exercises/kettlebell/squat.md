The kettlebell squat, usually called a goblet squat, holds one kettlebell against your chest. The weight in front acts as a counterbalance, so it is easy to stay upright. It works the thighs and glutes.

### How to do it
1. Hold a kettlebell by the horns (the sides of the handle) with both hands against your chest.
2. Stand with your feet a little wider than your hips and your toes turned slightly out.
3. Keep your elbows pointing down and your chest tall.
4. Bend your hips and knees to sit down between your legs.
5. Let your elbows lightly touch the inside of your knees at the bottom, with your thighs about parallel to the floor.
6. Push through your feet to stand back up.

**Tips:** Keep the bell close to your chest so it does not pull you forward. Use the elbows against your knees as a cue to push your knees out. Choose a weight you can hold without your shoulders rounding.

### In daily life
Squatting with a weight at your chest is how you carry a toddler, a laundry basket or a bag of flour from the floor to waist height.

### Why it matters
The goblet position makes the squat easy to learn, and it can help beginners practise depth and an upright torso before moving to heavier lifts.
