The kettlebell snatch swings a kettlebell from between your legs to overhead in one movement. It combines the hip power of the swing with a pull and a lockout overhead. It builds shoulder strength, conditioning and grip.

### How to do it
1. Stand with a kettlebell on the floor in front of you, hinge and grip it with one hand.
2. Hike the kettlebell back between your legs, then drive your hips forward powerfully.
3. Let the bell rise close to your body, pulling your elbow back and up.
4. As it passes your chest, punch your hand up and let the bell flip around your wrist so it rests on your forearm.
5. Lock your arm overhead, then guide the bell back down and hinge as it swings back. Repeat all repetitions, then switch sides.

**Tips:** Keep the bell close to your body and use your hips, not your arm, for the power. Wear a wrist guard or learn the flip with a light bell if it slams your forearm. Keep the area clear and your core braced.

### In daily life
It uses the same hinge as picking up and swinging a heavy load into position.

### Why it matters
Combat, collision and running sports value conditioning and strong shoulders. The snatch is a fast way to build both.
