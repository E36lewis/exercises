The Turkish get-up is a slow, full-body exercise in which you go from lying on your back to standing while holding a kettlebell straight above you, then reverse the steps. It trains shoulder stability, the trunk, the hips and the legs in one movement.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back with a kettlebell in your right hand, arm straight up. Bend your right knee with that foot flat, and put your left arm and leg out at about 45 degrees.
2. Keeping your eyes on the bell, roll up onto your left forearm, then onto your left hand.
3. Press through your right foot and left hand to lift your hips.
4. Sweep your left leg back and under you until you are kneeling.
5. Lift your torso upright, then stand up, with the bell still locked out overhead.
6. Reverse each step carefully to return to the floor, then switch sides.

**Tips:** Learn the pattern with no weight, or with a shoe balanced on your fist, before using a kettlebell. Keep your arm vertical and your wrist straight. Pause in each position rather than rushing.

### In daily life
It rehearses getting up from the ground safely, such as after kneeling in the garden, playing with children or getting back up after a fall.

### Why it matters
Holding a weight steady overhead while changing position teaches the shoulder to stay stable and the trunk and hips to work together, which can help body control and confidence.
