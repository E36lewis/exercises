The alternate lateral pulldown uses a resistance band anchored overhead to train your back one arm at a time. It mainly works the latissimus dorsi, the large muscle that sweeps from your spine to your upper arm, with help from your upper back, rear shoulders and biceps.

### How to do it
1. Anchor a resistance band securely above head height.
2. Stand or kneel facing the anchor and take an end of the band in each hand, arms extended overhead.
3. Brace your abdominals and keep your ribs down.
4. Pull one hand down toward your shoulder, driving your elbow toward your ribs.
5. Squeeze that shoulder blade down and back at the bottom.
6. Slowly let the arm rise until it is straight again, then repeat with the other arm.

**Tips:** Start the pull with your back, not by yanking with your hand. Keep your shoulders away from your ears and avoid leaning back. Check the band for nicks and make sure the anchor is secure before you pull.

### In daily life
The downward pull mirrors yanking a garage door down, pulling a blind cord or hauling yourself up onto a ledge.

### Why it matters
Working one arm at a time shows up any difference between your two sides, and a strong back can help balance the pushing work done by the chest and shoulders.
