The banded core twist uses a resistance band anchored to one side to train your trunk to turn against resistance. It works the obliques, the muscles on the sides of your waist, with the abdominals and hip flexors helping.

### How to do it
1. Anchor a band at chest height to a secure post.
2. Stand side-on to the anchor with your feet shoulder-width apart, holding the band with both hands in front of your chest.
3. Brace your abdominals.
4. Rotate your torso away from the anchor, keeping your arms extended.
5. Pause, then slowly return to the start.
6. Complete your repetitions and switch sides.

**Tips:** Turn from your trunk and keep your hips mostly facing forward. Control the return so the band does not pull you. Check the anchor is secure.

### In daily life
Turning against a load is what you do when you twist to put a bag on the back seat of a car or swing a golf club or a tennis racket.

### Why it matters
Controlled, loaded twisting can help build trunk strength and teach your core to absorb rotation, which supports your spine in everyday and sporting turns.
