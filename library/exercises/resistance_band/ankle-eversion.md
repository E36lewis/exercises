Ankle eversion with a resistance band turns the sole of your foot outward against light resistance. It trains the peroneal muscles on the outside of your lower leg. These muscles help stop your ankle from rolling inward in a sprain.

### How to do it
1. Sit on the floor with your legs straight, or on a chair with your foot supported.
2. Loop a resistance band around the forefoot of the working foot and hold the other end, or tie it to a fixed object on the inside of your foot.
3. Keep your leg still and slowly turn your foot outward against the band.
4. Pause at the end of the range.
5. Slowly return to the start. Repeat for all repetitions, then switch sides.

**Tips:** Move only at the ankle and keep your knee and hip still. Go slowly, especially on the return. Use a light band at first, because the peroneals are small muscles.

### In daily life
It helps your ankles stay steady on uneven pavement, curbs and stairs.

### Why it matters
Lateral ankle sprains are common in court, field, running, skating, dancing, gymnastics and climbing sports. Stronger peroneals can help the ankle resist twisting inward, and it is a standard step in ankle rehabilitation.
