A one-legged sideways hop with a resistance band around your legs. It trains the gluteus medius (the muscle on the outside of the hip that keeps your pelvis level), with the thighs and hamstrings helping you push off and land.

### How to do it
1. Put a loop band around both legs just above the knees.
2. Stand on one leg with a soft knee and your trunk upright.
3. Hop sideways a short distance, pushing off your standing foot.
4. Land on the other leg with the knee bent, and hold your balance for a moment.
5. Hop back and repeat for a set number of hops.
6. Switch legs and repeat.

**Tips:** Keep your knee over your foot on every landing and your pelvis level. Land softly. Start with small hops on a non-slip surface to protect your ankles and knees.

### In daily life
It prepares you for sudden sideways moves on one foot, such as stepping off a kerb, dodging a cyclist or catching your balance on uneven ground.

### Why it matters
Single-leg landings against band tension can help train the hip muscles that keep the knee steady, and they support change-of-direction sports.
