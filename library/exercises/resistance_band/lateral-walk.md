A sideways walk with a resistance band around your legs. It works the muscles on the outside of the hip that move your leg away from the midline (gluteus medius and minimus, and the tensor fasciae latae), with the glutes and hip rotators helping.

### How to do it
1. Place a loop band around your legs just above the knees or around your ankles.
2. Stand with feet hip-width apart and bend your knees slightly into a quarter squat.
3. Keep your chest up and your toes pointing forward.
4. Step one foot out to the side.
5. Follow with the other foot, keeping tension on the band.
6. Take a set number of steps in one direction, then return the other way.

**Tips:** Take small steps and don't let your feet touch, so the band stays tight. Keep your knees pushed out, not collapsing inward. Move slowly and don't lean your trunk from side to side.

### In daily life
You sidestep like this when you shuffle along a crowded row of seats or edge along a narrow ledge, and your hips work to keep your knees steady.

### Why it matters
A stable pelvis is a base for walking and running, and strengthening the hip abductors can help with knee control and support injury resilience.
