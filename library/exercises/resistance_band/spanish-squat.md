The Spanish squat is an isometric squat in which you sit back against a strong band that is looped behind your knees. You hold the bottom position for time. It loads the quadriceps and the patellar tendon heavily with little pain, so it is common in tendon rehabilitation.

### How to do it
1. Anchor a strong resistance band to a sturdy post or rack at about knee height.
2. Loop the band behind both knees and step back until the band is tight.
3. With your feet about shoulder-width apart, sit back and lower your hips until your knees are bent at about 60 degrees and your shins are upright.
4. Hold the position for the set time, for example 30 to 45 seconds.
5. Stand back up, step toward the anchor and rest. Repeat for the planned sets.

**Tips:** Keep your shins vertical and your weight through your whole foot. Make sure the anchor is secure and the band is not frayed. Stop if the pain gets worse during the hold.

### In daily life
It is the position of sitting down into a chair and holding it, with no knee strain.

### Why it matters
Court, snow, running and skating sports load the knee tendon with repeated jumps and stops. Isometric holds like this can reduce pain and keep the tendon loaded when other exercises are too sore.
