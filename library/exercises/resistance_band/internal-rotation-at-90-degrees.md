Internal rotation at 90 degrees is a resistance band exercise in which your upper arm is raised to shoulder height, elbow bent at a right angle, and you rotate your forearm forward and down. This is the throwing position. It trains the subscapularis and the chest and back muscles that power throwing and serving.

### How to do it
1. Anchor a resistance band at about shoulder height and stand side-on to the anchor.
2. Hold the band in the hand farthest from the anchor, lift your elbow out to shoulder height and bend it to 90 degrees, with your forearm pointing up.
3. Keeping your upper arm still, rotate your forearm forward and down.
4. Pause at the end of the range.
5. Return slowly. Repeat for all repetitions, then switch arms.

**Tips:** Keep your elbow at shoulder height and your upper arm still. Move slowly, and use a light band. Do not lean your trunk to get more range.

### In daily life
It is the motion of reaching up and then pulling something forward and down, such as slamming a door.

### Why it matters
Baseball and softball throwing, tennis serves and swimming strokes depend on fast internal rotation. Pair it with external rotation work so the shoulder stays balanced.
