External rotation at 90 degrees is a resistance band exercise in which your upper arm is raised to shoulder height, elbow bent at a right angle, and you rotate your forearm upward. This is the throwing position. It trains the rotator cuff muscles at the back of your shoulder.

### How to do it
1. Anchor a resistance band at about shoulder height and face the anchor.
2. Hold the band in one hand, lift your elbow out to the side to shoulder height and bend it to 90 degrees, with your hand in front of your shoulder.
3. Keeping your upper arm still, slowly rotate your forearm upward until it points toward the ceiling.
4. Pause, then lower slowly back to the start.
5. Repeat for all repetitions, then switch arms.

**Tips:** Keep your elbow at shoulder height and do not let it drop or move forward. Use a light band and move slowly, without arching your back. Stop if you feel a pinch at the front of your shoulder.

### In daily life
It is the motion of reaching overhead to a high shelf and returning your arm smoothly.

### Why it matters
Baseball and softball batting and throwing, racket sports and swimming all use the shoulder in this position. The rotator cuff must slow down the arm after each throw or stroke, so a stronger cuff helps protect the shoulder.
