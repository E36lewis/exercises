This is a shoulder exercise with a resistance band. You turn your forearm outward while your elbow stays at your side. It strengthens the rotator cuff muscles at the back of the shoulder blade, mainly the infraspinatus and teres minor, with the rear part of the deltoid helping.

### How to do it
1. Attach a resistance band to a fixed point at elbow height.
2. Stand sideways to the anchor, holding the band in the hand farthest from it.
3. Tuck your elbow against your side, bent to about 90 degrees, with your forearm across your belly.
4. Keeping the elbow in place, rotate your forearm outward, away from your belly.
5. Pause briefly, then return slowly.
6. Repeat, then switch sides.

**Tips:** Keep a small rolled towel between your elbow and side to help you stay tucked. Don't twist your torso. Use light tension and move slowly to protect the shoulder.

### In daily life
Opening a heavy door outward, pulling a drawer open with a turn of the arm and reaching back for a seat belt all use outward rotation. So does the follow-through of a throw.

### Why it matters
The rotator cuff holds the ball of the upper arm snugly in its socket. Strong external rotators can help keep the shoulder centred and balanced during pressing and overhead work.
