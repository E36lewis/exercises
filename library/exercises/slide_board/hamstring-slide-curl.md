The hamstring slide curl is a bridge with the heels on a slide board. You pull your heels toward your hips and slide them back out. The hamstrings work hard as they lengthen, and no machine is needed.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back with your heels on a slide board or on sliders, knees bent and arms by your sides.
2. Lift your hips into a bridge so your body forms a line from shoulders to knees.
3. Keeping your hips up, slowly slide your heels away until your legs are nearly straight.
4. Pull your heels back toward your hips, bending your knees.
5. Lower your hips and rest, then repeat.

**Tips:** Take about 3 seconds on the way out, because the lowering phase builds hamstring strength and protects against strains. Do 3 sets of 6 to 10 reps. If the legs are too long a lever, shorten the slide. Don't let your hips sag or your back arch. Stop if you feel a sharp pull at the back of the thigh.

### In daily life
Pulling yourself up from a low surface with your feet and getting out of a low chair with your legs both lean on the hamstrings.

### Why it matters
Field, court, run and collision sports all see many hamstring strains during sprints and cuts. Strong hamstrings that work well as they lengthen help prevent these injuries. This exercise builds that strength with only a slide board.
