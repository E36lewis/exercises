The body saw is a long-lever plank. In a forearm plank with your feet on a slide board, you slide your body back and forth a short way. The trunk muscles must resist the pull of the long lever, so it is harder than a standard plank.

### How to do it
1. Place a slide board on the floor, or use sliders under your toes.
2. Get into a forearm plank with elbows under your shoulders and feet on the sliding surface.
3. Brace your trunk and squeeze your glutes so your body forms a straight line.
4. Shift your body backward by pushing your forearms, letting your feet slide forward a few centimetres.
5. Slide forward again, past the starting position, as far as you can control.
6. Repeat in a slow, smooth rhythm.

**Tips:** Don't let your lower back sag or your hips pike up. Start with a short slide of 10 to 15 cm, and lengthen it as you improve. Do 3 sets of 8 to 12 slow slides, or 20 to 40 seconds of timed work. Stop if your back starts to ache.

### In daily life
Holding your trunk firm while you reach across a counter, push a heavy object across the floor or brace against a push all use this.

### Why it matters
Water, combat, gymnastics and rock-climbing athletes need a trunk that stays firm while arms and legs move. The body saw builds that stiffness over a long lever, which transfers to swimming, grappling, holding a body line on rings and keeping the hips close to the wall.
