The slide board lateral glide copies the side-to-side push of a speed skater on a low-friction board. You push off one foot, glide to the other side and stop on the bumper. It is low impact and works the glutes and inner thighs hard.

### How to do it
1. Place a slide board on a flat floor and put on booties or socks that slide.
2. Stand at one end with your weight on one leg, knee bent and chest leaning slightly forward.
3. Push off sideways with the standing leg and glide across the board.
4. Land on the opposite foot, bend the knee and reach the other leg across behind you, as skaters do.
5. Stop at the bumper and push back the other way in one smooth movement.
6. Swing your arms across your body for balance and rhythm.

**Tips:** Typical work is 30 to 60 seconds of gliding with 30 to 60 seconds of rest, for 6 to 10 rounds, or 3 x 3 minutes at a steady pace. Start slowly and keep your knees pointing in line with your toes. Use a board with end stops and keep the area clear of obstacles.

### In daily life
Sidestepping along a narrow ledge, dodging an obstacle on a path or recovering your balance on a slippery surface uses the same side push.

### Why it matters
Skating sports use this motion for thousands of strides. The board lets skaters train the exact stride on dry land, with the same push, glide and recovery, and it builds endurance in the muscles that matter without any impact on the joints.
