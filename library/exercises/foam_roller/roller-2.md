This is a foam roller drill that uses your body weight. It targets the long muscles beside your spine and the lats (latissimus dorsi), the broad muscles that run from the mid-back up to the arm. It can help release tension, but it is not a strength exercise.

### How to do it
1. Sit on the floor with a foam roller behind you.
2. Lie back so the roller rests across your mid-back, with your knees bent and feet flat.
3. Cross your arms over your chest and lift your hips a little off the floor.
4. Push through your feet to roll the roller slowly along your back.
5. To reach the lats, turn onto one side with the roller under the side of your upper back, near your armpit, and your arm extended overhead.
6. Roll gently a short distance, and stop on tight spots for a few breaths.
7. Lower down and step away.

**Tips:** Support your head and keep your neck relaxed. Control the pace and use your legs, not momentum. Don't press hard on the lower back, and stop at any sharp or pinching pain.

### In daily life
Carrying a child on one hip or lifting boxes can leave the sides of your back tight. Rolling the lats can make it more comfortable to reach overhead and twist to reach something behind you.

### Why it matters
Gentle rolling can help ease muscle tension, and it supports mobility through the back and shoulders. That can make training and everyday movement feel smoother.
