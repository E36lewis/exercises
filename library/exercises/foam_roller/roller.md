This is a self-massage and mobility drill. You lie on a foam roller and use your body weight to press the muscles along your spine, including the erector spinae (the long muscles beside your spine). It is a gentle way to work out tight spots, not a strength exercise.

### How to do it
1. Place a foam roller on the floor and sit in front of it.
2. Lie back so the roller sits across your upper back, just below your shoulder blades.
3. Bend your knees, plant your feet and cross your arms over your chest, or cradle your head in your hands.
4. Lift your hips slightly off the floor and press into your feet.
5. Roll slowly a short distance up and down, using your legs to move you.
6. When you find a tender spot, pause on it and breathe slowly for a few seconds.
7. Lower your hips and step away when finished.

**Tips:** Keep your core braced so your lower back doesn't sag. Move slowly rather than bouncing. Roll the upper and middle back, and avoid pressing hard on the lower spine. Stop if you feel sharp pain.

### In daily life
After a long drive or a day at a desk, your back can feel stiff and hunched. Rolling can make it easier to sit up tall and to turn your head and shoulders to check behind you.

### Why it matters
Rolling can help you feel looser before a workout and more settled afterwards. It supports movement through the upper back, which can help your posture over time.
