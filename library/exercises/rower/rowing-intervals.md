Rowing intervals alternate hard strokes on an indoor rower with easier recovery strokes. Each stroke uses the legs, trunk and arms in turn. It is full-body conditioning with no impact.

### How to do it
1. Sit on the rower with your feet strapped in and the handle held with an overhand grip. Set the damper at 3 to 5 for most people.
2. At the catch, shins are vertical, arms straight and your trunk leaning slightly forward.
3. Drive with your legs first, then lean the trunk back slightly, and finally pull the handle to your lower chest.
4. Return in reverse: arms out first, trunk forward, then bend the knees to slide up.
5. Repeat in a smooth rhythm of one count on the drive and two on the recovery.
6. Row hard for the work period, then row easily for the rest.

**Tips:** Common formats are 8 x 250 m with 1 minute of easy rowing between, 5 x 500 m, or 10 x 1 minute hard with 1 minute easy. Aim for 20 to 28 strokes per minute. Keep your back flat, not rounded, and don't pull with your arms first. Check that your feet are strapped in firmly.

### In daily life
Pulling a boat to shore, hauling a heavy bag up a step or dragging a load toward you uses the same leg-trunk-arm sequence.

### Why it matters
Water sports such as rowing, paddling and swimming use this pull pattern directly. Gymnastics and rock-climbing athletes get a low-impact way to build heart and lung fitness while also training the pulling muscles of the back and arms, which suits days when they want to spare the joints.
