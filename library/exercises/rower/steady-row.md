The steady row is continuous, easy to moderate rowing on an indoor rower. Each stroke links legs, trunk and arms. It builds the aerobic base with no impact.

### How to do it
1. Sit on the rower, strap in your feet and hold the handle with an overhand grip. Set the damper at 3 to 5.
2. Start at the catch with shins vertical, arms straight and trunk leaning forward slightly.
3. Push with your legs first, then lean back slightly and pull the handle to your lower chest.
4. Reverse the order on the way back: arms, trunk, then knees.
5. Take a count of one on the drive and two on the way back.
6. Settle into 18 to 24 strokes per minute and keep this rhythm.

**Tips:** A usual session is 20 to 60 minutes in zone 2, an effort at which you can talk in short sentences, or for example 3 x 10 minutes with 2 minutes' rest. Keep your back flat, your shoulders relaxed and your grip loose. Don't yank the handle with your arms.

### In daily life
Hauling a rope, paddling a canoe and pulling a heavy load along the ground use the same pulling sequence.

### Why it matters
Water sports train this stroke directly, and gymnastics and rock-climbing athletes build a low-impact aerobic base that supports the harder skill and strength sessions.
