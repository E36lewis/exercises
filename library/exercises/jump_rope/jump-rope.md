Jump rope is repeated, light jumping while turning a rope under your feet. The calves and feet bounce, and the wrists, not the arms, turn the rope. It builds footwork, rhythm and calf conditioning.

### How to do it
1. Pick a rope that reaches your armpits when you stand on the middle with both feet.
2. Hold the handles at hip height with your elbows near your sides.
3. Turn the rope with your wrists and forearms.
4. Jump just high enough, about 2 to 3 cm, to clear the rope, landing on the balls of your feet.
5. Keep your knees slightly bent and your jumps small and light.
6. Find a steady rhythm and breathe easily.

**Tips:** A usual plan is 5 to 10 rounds of 1 to 3 minutes with 30 to 60 seconds' rest, or intervals of 30 seconds on and 30 seconds off. Use a firm, flat surface and good shoes. Build up gradually, because the calves and Achilles tendons are loaded far more than you may expect.

### In daily life
Skipping with children, hopping over a puddle repeatedly and a quick bounce on your toes to stay warm all use the same bounce.

### Why it matters
Combat sports use rope work for footwork, rhythm and endurance. Gymnastics and rock-climbing athletes use it for light, quick feet and calf conditioning, and it is easy to take anywhere.
