An abdominal exercise from your knees, rolling a stability ball forward and back with your forearms on it. It works the rectus abdominis, with the shoulders, lats and hip flexors helping and your back muscles keeping your spine steady.

### How to do it
1. Kneel behind a stability ball with your forearms on top of it.
2. Brace your abdomen, tuck your pelvis slightly and keep your back flat.
3. Roll the ball forward as you extend your body.
4. Go only as far as you can keep your back from sagging.
5. Pause.
6. Pull the ball back toward your knees by squeezing your abs.

**Tips:** Don't let your lower back sag. Move slowly, and start with a short range. Stop if you feel pain in your back or shoulders.

### In daily life
Reaching forward from a kneeling position, such as washing a floor or reaching under a bed, needs a trunk that holds firm as your arms extend.

### Why it matters
The abdominals have to resist your body weight pulling your hips down, which can build strong core control for lifting and reaching.
