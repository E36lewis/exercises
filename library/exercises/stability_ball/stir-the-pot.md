Stir the pot is a plank variation in which your forearms rest on a stability ball and you move the ball in small circles. The ball is unstable, so the abdominals work hard to stop your trunk from twisting or sagging. It trains anti-rotation and anti-extension strength together.

### How to do it
1. Place a stability ball on the floor and kneel behind it, then rest your forearms on the ball.
2. Step your feet back into a plank, with your body in a straight line from head to heels.
3. Brace your abs and squeeze your glutes.
4. Use your forearms to move the ball in small circles, as if stirring a pot, keeping your hips still.
5. Do several circles in one direction, then switch direction.

**Tips:** Keep your hips level and your lower back from sagging. Start with small circles and a wider foot stance if it feels too hard. Make sure the area is clear in case the ball rolls away.

### In daily life
It builds the trunk control for holding yourself steady while reaching or pushing with your arms.

### Why it matters
Swimmers, gymnasts and climbers need a trunk that stays stiff while the arms move. The moving ball makes this plank much harder than a floor plank.
