A push-up with your hands on top of a stability ball. It works the chest and shoulders as a normal push-up does, and the ball's wobble makes your core, shoulder blades and arms work harder to keep you steady.

### How to do it
1. Place your hands on the top of the ball, a little wider than shoulder-width, and walk your feet back.
2. Make a straight line from head to heels.
3. Brace your abdomen and glutes.
4. Lower your chest toward the ball, with your elbows about 45 degrees from your body.
5. Stop when your chest is near the ball.
6. Press back up, keeping the ball still.

**Tips:** Keep your body rigid and your wrists stacked under your shoulders. A wider foot stance is more stable. Use a clear space, and stop if the ball starts to roll away.

### In daily life
Pushing on something that moves, such as leaning on a rolling cart or a wobbly railing, needs your shoulders to adjust as you go.

### Why it matters
The unstable surface asks the small muscles around the shoulder blade to work, which can build control as well as strength.
