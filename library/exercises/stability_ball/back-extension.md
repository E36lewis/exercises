The stability-ball back extension strengthens the muscles along your spine (the erector spinae), with the glutes and hamstrings helping. The ball makes the movement smooth and a little unstable, so your trunk has to stay alert.

### How to do it
1. Lie face down over a stability ball with your hips on the ball and your feet braced on the floor, wide for stability.
2. Cross your arms over your chest or place your hands lightly behind your head.
3. Let your upper body curl gently over the ball.
4. Squeeze your glutes and raise your chest until your body forms a straight line.
5. Pause briefly at the top.
6. Lower back down slowly.

**Tips:** Lift only until your body is straight, without arching further. Keep your neck in line with your spine. Move slowly, and stop if you feel pain in your lower back.

### In daily life
The muscles that lift you from a forward bend are the ones you use to straighten up after weeding the garden or tying a shoe.

### Why it matters
Training these muscles to work for longer can help build back endurance, which supports good posture and a strong lower back during long hours standing or sitting.
