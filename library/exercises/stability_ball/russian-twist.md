This is a rotating core exercise performed with your upper back on a stability ball. You hold your arms above your chest and turn them from side to side while your hips stay high. It works the obliques and abs, with the lower-back and hip muscles keeping you steady.

### How to do it
1. Sit on a stability ball and walk your feet forward until your upper back rests on the ball.
2. Plant your feet wide and lift your hips so your body forms a line from shoulders to knees.
3. Extend your arms above your chest, hands together.
4. Rotate your arms and torso to one side.
5. Return through the centre and rotate to the other side.
6. Keep your hips high and your feet planted.

**Tips:** Move slowly so the ball stays steady. Don't let your hips sag. Widen your stance if you wobble, and clear some space around you.

### In daily life
Turning to reach something while keeping your footing, such as on a moving train, uses the same trunk control and balance.

### Why it matters
An unstable surface asks your trunk muscles to work harder to keep you still. That supports balance and control of your spine.
