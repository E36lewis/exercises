Glute bridge marches are done lying on your back with your heels on a stability ball. You hold a bridge and lift one foot at a time, so your glutes keep your hips up while your abdomen stops your pelvis from tipping.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back with your arms by your sides and your heels on top of the ball, knees bent.
2. Press your heels into the ball and lift your hips until your body makes a line from shoulders to knees.
3. Tighten your abdomen and squeeze your glutes.
4. Slowly lift one foot a short way off the ball, as if marching.
5. Put it back, then lift the other foot.
6. Keep your hips level and in the bridge position throughout.
7. Lower your hips when you finish the set.

**Tips:** Don't let your hips drop or twist when a foot leaves the ball. Move slowly. If the ball feels unsteady, set it against a wall.

### In daily life
Your hips stay level when you walk and shift weight from one leg to the other. This exercise trains that, and it can feel like the steadiness you need on stairs.

### Why it matters
Stepping while your hips are lifted forces the glutes and trunk to work against rotation. That can help hip stability and give you control that carries over to single-leg tasks.
