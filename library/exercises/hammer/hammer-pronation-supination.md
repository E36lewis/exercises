Hammer pronation and supination turns your forearm so the palm faces down and then up, while you hold a hammer by the handle. The hammer's weight sits off center, so your forearm muscles must control it. It trains the muscles that rotate the forearm.

### How to do it
1. Sit with your forearm resting on your thigh or a bench, wrist just past the edge, holding a hammer by the end of the handle with the head pointing up.
2. Keep your elbow still against your leg and your wrist straight.
3. Slowly rotate your forearm so the palm faces down, letting the hammer head fall to the side.
4. Then rotate the other way until your palm faces up.
5. Repeat smoothly for all repetitions, then switch arms.

**Tips:** Hold the hammer near the end of the handle to start, then slide your hand up to make it easier. Keep the elbow tucked and the movement slow. Do not let the elbow swing.

### In daily life
It is the motion of turning a key, a doorknob or a screwdriver, and of pouring from a jug.

### Why it matters
Racket sports, bat sports, climbing, gymnastics and shooting sports load the forearm. Strong rotators and wrist muscles help protect against tendon pain at the elbow, such as tennis elbow.
