This is a front squat with a hammer held out in front of you in both hands, like a flag on a pole. The weight sits out ahead of your body, so your thighs, glutes and shoulders all work to keep it up while you squat.

### How to do it
1. Pick up a hammer by the end of its handle with both hands, so the head hangs out away from you.
2. Raise it until your arms point forward, roughly level with your chest.
3. Stand with your feet about shoulder-width apart and your toes turned out slightly.
4. Brace your abdomen and keep your ribs down.
5. Bend your hips and knees and lower until your thighs are about parallel to the floor, with your heels flat.
6. Keep your arms steady and your chest tall as you go down.
7. Push through your whole foot to stand back up.

**Tips:** Start with a light hammer, because the weight is far from your hands and feels heavier than it is. Keep your torso upright. Clear the space in front of you before you start.

### In daily life
Holding a load out in front of you while you lower yourself is the same job as setting down a bucket, a toolbox or a pot at floor height without leaning forward.

### Why it matters
The held weight pulls your torso forward, so your trunk and upper back work to stay tall. That can help build leg strength and the shoulder endurance to carry things at arm's length.
