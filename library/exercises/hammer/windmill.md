The hammer windmill is a shoulder, core and hip exercise in which you hold a light hammer or hammer-shaped weight overhead and bend sideways, sliding your free hand down your leg. It works the shoulders that hold the weight up and the trunk muscles that control the lean.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet a little wider than shoulder-width apart, with your toes turned slightly to one side.
2. Hold the hammer overhead with one arm straight and your wrist in line with your forearm.
3. Look at the hammer as you push your hip out to the side and lean your trunk the other way.
4. Slide your free hand down your leg as far as feels comfortable.
5. Squeeze your glutes and the side of your trunk to return to standing, then switch sides.

**Tips:** Choose a hammer light enough for you to hold steady. Keep your arm straight and the hammer above your shoulder. Move slowly and stay clear of anyone nearby.

### In daily life
It resembles reaching to the side to pick something up while keeping one hand raised, as when you reach for a low item while holding a shelf.

### Why it matters
Holding an unusual weight overhead while moving makes the shoulder and trunk work together to keep you balanced.
