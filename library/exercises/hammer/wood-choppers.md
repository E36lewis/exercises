Wood choppers use a hammer or similar weight to copy the motion of chopping wood. You swing the weight from above one shoulder down across your body, which works the obliques and the deep core muscles that turn and brace the trunk.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet about shoulder-width apart and your knees slightly bent.
2. Hold the hammer handle with both hands and raise it overhead, slightly to one side.
3. Swing it down diagonally across your body toward the opposite hip.
4. Rotate your trunk and pivot your back foot as you chop.
5. Raise the hammer back overhead along the same path.
6. Finish your set, then repeat on the other side.

**Tips:** Brace your abs so the work comes from your trunk and not your lower back. Use a controlled swing rather than a violent one. Make sure there is plenty of space around you, and check that the head of the hammer is secure.

### In daily life
It resembles splitting logs, shovelling snow, or twisting to put something down behind you.

### Why it matters
Swinging diagonally trains the obliques to produce and control rotation, which supports the trunk during many throwing, swinging and lifting tasks.
