Hammer radial deviation moves your wrist sideways, toward the thumb, while you hold a hammer. The hammer's weight sits off center, so the wrist muscles must control it. It trains wrist stability.

### How to do it
1. Sit with your forearm on your thigh or a bench, hand past the edge and your thumb up, holding a hammer by the end of the handle with the head pointing up.
2. Let the hammer head tilt slightly down toward the floor.
3. Slowly raise the head of the hammer toward the ceiling by bending your wrist toward your thumb.
4. Pause at the top.
5. Lower slowly. Repeat for all repetitions, then switch arms.

**Tips:** Keep your forearm still and only move your wrist. Hold the hammer closer to the head to make it lighter, and further down the handle to make it harder. Move slowly.

### In daily life
It builds the wrist strength for hammering, gardening and using tools.

### Why it matters
Racket, precision and bat sports rely on a stable, strong wrist. Training it from each side helps protect against overuse injuries.
