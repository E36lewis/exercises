The hanging knee raise is the easier version of the hanging leg raise. While hanging from a bar you bend your knees and lift them toward your chest. It works the abdominals and hip flexors and also builds grip.

### How to do it
1. Hang from a pull up bar with an overhand grip, hands shoulder-width apart and arms straight.
2. Pull your shoulders gently down and away from your ears.
3. Brace your trunk and bend your knees, lifting them toward your chest.
4. Tilt your pelvis up at the top so your lower back rounds slightly.
5. Lower your legs slowly, without swinging, back to the hanging position.

**Tips:** Control the way down, because it is where the abdominals work hardest. Do 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps. Avoid swinging. If your grip fails first, use straps. When this feels easy, progress to straight legs or toes to bar.

### In daily life
Lifting your knees to climb onto a high step, pulling yourself up into a truck bed and getting into a tall saddle use the same hip flexor and trunk action.

### Why it matters
Gymnasts and rock climbers need to bring the hips and legs up toward a bar or a wall, and to stop the body swinging when hanging. This builds trunk and grip strength with a movement they can control easily.
