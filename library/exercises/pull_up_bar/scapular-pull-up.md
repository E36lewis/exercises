The scapular pull up is a small movement on a bar with straight arms. You pull your shoulder blades down and together, which lifts the body a few centimetres. It teaches the shoulder position needed for every pull-up and hang.

### How to do it
1. Hang from a pull up bar with an overhand grip, hands just wider than your shoulders and arms straight.
2. Let your shoulders rise toward your ears slightly.
3. Without bending your elbows, pull your shoulder blades down and back so your chest rises a few centimetres.
4. Hold for a second.
5. Lower slowly back to the hanging position.

**Tips:** Keep your elbows straight and your body still. Do 3 sets of 6 to 10 reps, or hold the top for 3 to 5 seconds. Don't swing. If your grip gives out first, use straps or a lower bar with your feet on the floor.

### In daily life
Pulling yourself up onto a ledge, steadying your shoulders when carrying a heavy bag and hanging from a rail all rely on this shoulder position.

### Why it matters
Gymnastics, rock-climbing and water sports all depend on healthy, well-controlled shoulders when pulling or hanging. Learning to depress the shoulder blades first protects the joint and makes pull-ups more effective.
