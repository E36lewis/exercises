The seated calf raise is a machine exercise for the soleus, the deep calf muscle. With your knees bent and a weight on your thighs, you lift your heels as high as you can. Because your knees are bent, the soleus does most of the work rather than the gastrocnemius.

### How to do it
1. Sit on the machine with the balls of your feet on the platform and your thighs under the pad.
2. Adjust the pad so it sits snugly on your lower thighs.
3. Release the safety catch and lower your heels as far as is comfortable.
4. Press through the balls of your feet to rise as high as possible.
5. Pause at the top, lower slowly and set the safety catch when you finish.

**Tips:** Use a full range of motion and avoid bouncing at the bottom. Keep your knees pointing forward. Go slowly, especially down.

### In daily life
It helps your calves manage a long day on your feet and the push-off when you climb stairs.

### Why it matters
The soleus takes much of the load on the Achilles tendon in running, jumping and skating. Training it helps the lower leg tolerate high volumes of impact.
