Cable external rotation turns your forearm outward against a cable's steady pull. Your elbow stays at your side. It trains the rotator cuff muscles at the back of your shoulder, and the cable keeps the load even through the whole movement.

### How to do it
1. Set a cable pulley at elbow height and stand side-on to it, with the handle in the hand nearest the machine.
2. Tuck your elbow against your side with a small towel between your elbow and your ribs, and bend it to 90 degrees, with your forearm across your stomach.
3. Keeping your elbow against your side, rotate your forearm outward away from the machine.
4. Pause, then return slowly.
5. Repeat for all repetitions, then turn around and do the other arm.

**Tips:** Use a light weight and keep the elbow pinned to your side. Do not twist your trunk. Control the return, because the cable pulls you back.

### In daily life
It supports your shoulder when you reach out and back, such as when you throw a ball or reach behind you.

### Why it matters
Baseball, softball, racket sports and swimming rely on a strong rotator cuff to guide and slow the arm. A steady cable load builds that strength.
