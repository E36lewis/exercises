The landmine rotation, also called the landmine twist, turns a barbell anchored at one end through an arc in front of you. The hips and trunk create the turn and the arms guide the bar. The fixed pivot makes it easier on the shoulders than a free weight.

### How to do it
1. Wedge the end of an Olympic bar into a landmine sleeve or a corner. Load the free end with a light plate, about 5 to 10 kg to start.
2. Stand side-on to the anchor, feet slightly wider than your hips, and hold the free end of the bar in both hands at chest height with arms nearly straight.
3. Brace your trunk and soften your knees.
4. Rotate your hips and shoulders together and swing the bar in an arc to one side, pivoting on the back foot as you turn.
5. Stop at about hip height at the bottom of the arc, then pull the bar back across the front of your body to the other side.
6. Keep the arc smooth and let the hips lead. Finish all reps on one side, or alternate sides.

**Tips:** Move from the hips and trunk, not by swinging the arms. Keep the load light until you control the arc. Pivot your back foot so your knee isn't twisted. Typical work is 3 sets of 8 to 12 turns per side, or alternating sides for 30 to 45 seconds.

### In daily life
Turning to put a heavy box on a shelf beside you, swinging a bag of mulch into a cart, or twisting to throw all use this hip-led rotation.

### Why it matters
Bat, racket, precision and combat sports all turn the trunk to create power for a swing, a strike or a throw. This builds that rotational power through the trunk and hips, and the fixed bar path keeps the shoulders safe, so it suits athletes who swing or strike many times a week.
