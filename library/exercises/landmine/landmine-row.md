The landmine row pulls the free end of an anchored barbell toward your chest while you hinge forward. It builds a heavy, strong back and needs no bench.

### How to do it
1. Wedge one end of an Olympic bar into a landmine sleeve and load the other end with plates.
2. Stand over the bar, feet shoulder-width apart, and hinge at the hips with your knees soft.
3. Grip the bar just behind the plates, using a V-handle if you have one.
4. Keep your back flat and pull the bar toward your lower chest.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together, then lower the bar slowly.

**Tips:** Do 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps. Don't jerk the weight up or round your back. Keep your neck in line with your spine. Make sure the sleeve is secure and the plates are on firmly.

### In daily life
Pulling a heavy bag out of a car, starting a lawnmower and hauling a load toward you use the same hinge and pull.

### Why it matters
Collision, combat and water sports reward a strong back for gripping, pulling and rowing. The landmine row lets athletes row heavy in a fixed arc that is kind to the lower back.
