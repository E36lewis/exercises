The landmine press pushes the free end of an anchored barbell up and forward at an angle. The slanted bar path suits the natural movement of the shoulder blade, so it is kinder to the shoulder than a straight overhead press.

### How to do it
1. Wedge one end of an Olympic bar into a landmine sleeve or a corner and load the other end with a plate.
2. Stand facing the bar, feet hip-width apart or in a split stance, with the end of the bar at your shoulder.
3. Hold the end in one or both hands against your chest.
4. Brace your trunk and press the bar up and forward until your arm is straight.
5. Reach slightly at the top, so your shoulder blade moves forward.
6. Lower the bar slowly to your shoulder.

**Tips:** Do 3 to 4 sets of 6 to 10 reps per arm. Keep your ribs down and don't lean back. Start with a light load, since the bar itself is heavy. Lock the sleeve so the bar can't slip out.

### In daily life
Pushing a heavy door away from you, putting a bag onto a high shelf at an angle and pressing a child up to your shoulder use the same push.

### Why it matters
Bat, racket, collision and combat sports involve a lot of pushing and throwing. The landmine press builds that strength in a shoulder-friendly way, which makes it useful for athletes with sore shoulders.
