The box jump is a two-footed jump from the floor onto a sturdy box. You swing your arms, push hard through your legs and land softly on top. Landing high keeps the impact low, so it trains power with less stress than jumping down.

### How to do it
1. Set a stable plyo box at 30 to 50 cm. Start low and raise it only when your landings are quiet and balanced.
2. Stand about an arm's length from the box with feet hip-width apart.
3. Swing your arms back and bend your hips and knees into a quarter squat.
4. Swing your arms up and jump, driving through the whole foot, and land on the middle of the box with both feet.
5. Land softly, hips back and knees bent, knees over your toes.
6. Stand up fully on the box, then step down one foot at a time. Reset and repeat.

**Tips:** Step down, don't jump down. Repeated drops onto the floor add a lot of impact and are a common cause of sore Achilles tendons and shins. Make sure the box can't slide and wear flat, grippy shoes. Aim for 3 to 5 sets of 3 to 5 jumps with full rest, because the goal is a fast, crisp jump, not tiredness.

### In daily life
Jumping onto a loading dock, hopping up a high step or clearing a low wall uses this same hip, knee and ankle drive.

### Why it matters
Power and speed matter in almost every sport, from sprinting and jumping in court and field sports to explosive starts in combat sports and snow sports. Because you land high on the box, the impact is low, so it is a safe way for most athletes to train leg power.
