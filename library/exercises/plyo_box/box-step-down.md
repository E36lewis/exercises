The box step down is a slow, controlled lowering from a box on one leg. The standing leg bends while the other heel taps the floor. It trains the knee to control a descent, like walking down a hill.

### How to do it
1. Stand on a plyo box at 20 to 30 cm, with one foot on the box and the other hanging off the edge.
2. Hold your arms in front of you for balance, or touch a wall.
3. Slowly bend the standing knee and lower the free heel to the floor, taking 3 seconds.
4. Keep your knee over your toes and your hips level.
5. Tap the floor lightly, then push through the standing foot to stand up.
6. Finish all reps on one leg, then switch.

**Tips:** Do 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps on each leg. Start with a lower box. Move slowly, and if the knee drops inward, reduce the height. Make sure the box is firm and doesn't slide. Stop if you feel sharp knee pain.

### In daily life
Walking down stairs, stepping down from a curb and descending a steep trail all require this control.

### Why it matters
Running, hiking, snow and court sports involve a lot of lowering through the knee, on downhills and landings. Slow step downs build the eccentric strength that protects the knee.
