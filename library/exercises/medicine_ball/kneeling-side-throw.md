A rotational power exercise with a medicine ball, thrown from a kneeling position. Kneeling takes your legs and feet out of the movement, so your obliques (the side abdominal muscles) and hips have to produce the force.

### How to do it
1. Kneel on a mat on both knees, hips tall, holding a medicine ball at one hip. Face side-on to a sturdy wall or a partner about an arm's length or two away.
2. Brace your abdomen and keep your chest lifted.
3. Turn your torso away from the wall to load the ball behind your hip.
4. Rotate quickly toward the wall, driving from your hips and trunk.
5. Release the ball with both hands, letting your arms follow the turn.
6. Catch the rebound, reset, and repeat. Then switch sides.

**Tips:** Power comes from the trunk and hips, not the arms. Keep your hips square over your knees instead of leaning back. Choose a ball light enough that you stay upright and in control.

### In daily life
The same twist-and-release helps when you swing a heavy bag from the ground into a boot, or toss a bundle of laundry across a room.

### Why it matters
Practising fast, controlled rotation can help your trunk produce and absorb twisting force, which supports throwing and swinging sports.
