The standing side throw is a medicine ball exercise that builds rotational power. You turn your hips and trunk and throw the ball sideways to a wall or partner. The obliques and hips power the throw, with the legs pushing off the floor.

### How to do it
1. Stand sideways to a wall or partner, with your feet shoulder-width apart and your knees soft.
2. Hold the ball at your hip on the side away from the wall.
3. Turn your hips and shoulders away from the wall to load up.
4. Rotate quickly toward the target, pushing through your back foot, and release the ball.
5. Catch the rebound with both hands, and absorb it by turning back.
6. Complete your reps, then face the other way.

**Tips:** Let the turn start from the hips and drive through the trunk rather than just your arms. Keep your feet planted. Stay a safe distance from the wall, and pick a ball that bounces back well.

### In daily life
Turning and throwing sideways is the movement of tossing a bag into the back seat or turning to lift a box and place it beside you.

### Why it matters
Practising the rotating throw can help build trunk power and strength for sports with swinging or throwing, such as tennis, golf or baseball.
