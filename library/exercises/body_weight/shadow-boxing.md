Shadow boxing is throwing punches and moving as if you had an opponent, without a bag or a partner. It trains punching technique, footwork and conditioning.

### How to do it
1. Stand in your fighting stance, with your knees soft and your hands up by your chin.
2. Throw a jab and cross, rotating from the hips and the back foot.
3. Add hooks, uppercuts, slips and steps in all directions.
4. Keep moving, bounce on the balls of your feet and breathe out as you punch.
5. Picture an opponent and return your hands to guard after each punch.
6. Work in rounds of 2 to 3 minutes, with 30 to 60 seconds rest, for 3 to 6 rounds.

**Tips:** Snap punches and don't lock out the elbow. Keep your chin down and hands up. Slow the speed first to get form right.

### In daily life
Keeping the guard up and moving builds general stamina for daily activity.

### Why it matters
Boxers and martial artists use it to polish technique and for warm-ups and conditioning, with no equipment.
