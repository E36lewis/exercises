A two-foot jump forward for distance. You swing your arms, drive your hips and land softly. It builds horizontal power and teaches you to absorb a landing.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet hip-width apart and your arms up.
2. Swing your arms back as you bend your hips and knees into a quarter squat.
3. Swing your arms forward and jump out as far as you can, extending your hips, knees and ankles.
4. Land on both feet at once, with your hips back and your knees bent.
5. Hold the landing for a moment.
6. Walk back and repeat.

**Tips:** Land softly and stay in control; if you tumble forward, jump shorter. Keep your knees over your toes on landing. Jump on a non-slip surface, and stop when your landings get sloppy.

### In daily life
It is the power you use to jump across a puddle or leap off a low wall.

### Why it matters
Sprinting, jumping and changing direction in every sport depend on how much force the hips and legs can produce quickly. A broad jump trains that push forward and the landing control that protects the knees.
