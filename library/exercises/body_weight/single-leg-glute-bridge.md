A bridge on one leg, with the other leg held up. It doubles the load on the glute and hamstring of the working side and makes the hips and trunk work to stay level.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat.
2. Lift one leg and straighten it, or hold the knee up toward your chest.
3. Brace your abdomen and push through the heel of the other foot.
4. Lift your hips until your body makes a straight line.
5. Keep your hips level, not tilted to the side.
6. Lower slowly. Do all your reps, then switch legs.

**Tips:** Don't let your hips drop on the free side. Keep your lower back relaxed, not arched. Return to the two-leg bridge if you can't keep your hips level.

### In daily life
It is like climbing stairs or stepping up a hill, where each leg must push your whole body weight.

### Why it matters
Most sports are played on one leg at a time. This lifts glute and hamstring strength without equipment and shows if one side is weaker than the other, which is useful for staying injury-free.
