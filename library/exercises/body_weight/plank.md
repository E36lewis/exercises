A hold on your forearms and toes with your body in a straight line. It is mainly an abdominal exercise: the rectus abdominis and obliques stop your hips from sagging, while the shoulders, glutes and thighs help keep you steady.

### How to do it
1. Lie face down, then place your forearms on the floor with your elbows under your shoulders.
2. Tuck your toes under and lift your body off the floor.
3. Make a straight line from head to heels.
4. Squeeze your glutes and brace your abdomen as if bracing for a light poke.
5. Hold, breathing steadily and keeping your gaze on the floor.
6. Lower to the floor to finish.

**Tips:** Don't let your hips sag or pike up. Keep your neck in line with your spine. End the set when you can no longer hold good form, and build the time up slowly. Drop to your knees to make it easier.

### In daily life
A plank trains the trunk to hold still while your arms and legs move, as when you push a stroller, carry a toddler or lean over a counter.

### Why it matters
Holding your trunk steady can help protect your lower back and supports good posture. It teaches you to brace before you lift or reach.
