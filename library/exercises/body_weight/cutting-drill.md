The cutting drill trains you to plant one foot and push off at about 45 degrees to change direction while running. It builds the leg strength and control used in cuts on the field and court.

### How to do it
1. Set up cones in a zigzag, each about 5 to 10 meters (5 to 10 yards) apart and offset to the left and right by 3 to 4 meters.
2. Sprint to the first cone at 70 to 80 percent effort.
3. Plant the outside foot beside the cone, with the knee bent and the chest over it.
4. Push hard off that foot at about 45 degrees toward the next cone.
5. Repeat through all cones, then walk back.
6. Do 4 to 6 runs, resting 45 to 60 seconds.

**Tips:** Plant with the knee over the foot and not collapsing in. Lower your hips before the cut. Increase speed gradually.

### In daily life
Dodging a person on the pavement is a small cut.

### Why it matters
Football, soccer, rugby, basketball and similar sports are full of cuts. Practicing the plant and push teaches the controlled mechanics that protect the knee and ankle.
