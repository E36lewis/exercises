A floor drill where you lower the opposite arm and leg while your lower back stays pressed down. It trains the deep abdominal muscles to hold your trunk still while your limbs move.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back with your arms straight up over your shoulders and your hips and knees bent to 90 degrees, shins parallel to the floor.
2. Press your lower back gently into the floor and brace your abdomen.
3. Slowly lower your right arm overhead and straighten your left leg toward the floor, without touching it.
4. Stop before your lower back lifts off the floor.
5. Return to the start and repeat on the other side.
6. Keep alternating, breathing out as you reach.

**Tips:** Move slowly. If your back arches, shorten the reach. Keep your ribs down and your neck relaxed. Start by moving only the legs if the full version is too hard.

### In daily life
Your trunk does this when you reach for something on a high shelf while keeping your balance, or roll over in bed without twisting your back.

### Why it matters
It builds the trunk control that every lift and sport rests on. It is a safe starting drill for all sports, and a gentle way to teach the abdominals to protect the lower back.
