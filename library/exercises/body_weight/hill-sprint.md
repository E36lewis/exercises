A hill sprint is a short, hard run uphill. The slope makes you lean forward and push, so it trains acceleration with less impact than sprinting on flat ground.

### How to do it
1. Find a smooth hill with a gentle slope, 6 to 10 percent, and mark 20 to 40 meters (about 20 to 40 yards).
2. Warm up with jogging and a few strides.
3. Run uphill at full effort, leaning forward and driving the knees.
4. Pump your arms and keep the steps quick.
5. Walk back down for recovery, about 60 to 90 seconds or more.
6. Do 6 to 10 reps.

**Tips:** Keep a tall posture with a slight forward lean from the ankles. Avoid overstriding. Warm up well and stop if the hamstrings tighten.

### In daily life
Climbing a hill or catching a train up a ramp uses this effort.

### Why it matters
Runners and field players use hills to build leg power with a lower risk of injury than flat sprints, and to practice a strong forward drive.
