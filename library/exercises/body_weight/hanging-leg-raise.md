The hanging leg raise lifts your legs in front of you while you hang from a bar. The hip flexors lift the legs and the abdomen stops your pelvis from tilting, while your grip and shoulders hold you up.

### How to do it
1. Hang from a bar with an overhand grip, arms straight, hands about shoulder-width apart.
2. Brace your abdomen and keep your shoulders pulled down.
3. Raise your legs in front of you, with your knees bent or straight, to about hip height or higher.
4. Curl your pelvis up slightly at the top to involve the abdomen.
5. Pause, then lower your legs slowly.
6. Stop any swinging before the next repeat.

**Tips:** Bend your knees to make it easier. Don't swing or kick. Hold the bar firmly, and stop if your grip tires.

### In daily life
Lifting your legs onto a high step or up into a vehicle shares the hip-flexing motion, and hanging builds a grip you also use for carrying.

### Why it matters
It trains the abdomen and hip flexors together with your grip and shoulders. That can help build trunk strength and support the control needed for climbing and gymnastic skills.
