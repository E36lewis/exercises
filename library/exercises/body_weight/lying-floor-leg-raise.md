A bodyweight core exercise lying on your back and lifting your legs. The hip flexors (including the psoas) do the lifting, while the rectus abdominis holds your pelvis and lower back steady against the floor.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back on a mat with your legs straight and your arms by your sides.
2. Press your lower back gently into the floor.
3. Keeping your legs together and nearly straight, lift them toward the ceiling.
4. Stop when your legs are vertical.
5. Lower your legs slowly, stopping before your lower back arches.
6. Repeat.

**Tips:** Keep your lower back flat. If it arches, bend your knees or lower your legs less. Don't swing your legs, and place your hands under your hips if it helps.

### In daily life
It copies lifting your legs into a car or onto a bed while your trunk stays still.

### Why it matters
Learning to keep the pelvis still while the legs move can help you control your lower back and supports good posture and movement.
