A calf exercise where you rise onto your toes on a step and slowly lower your heels below the step. The slow lowering loads the calf and the Achilles tendon, and is a standard tendon rehab and prevention drill.

### How to do it
1. Stand on the edge of a step on the balls of your feet, holding a rail for balance.
2. Rise up onto your toes with both feet.
3. Shift your weight onto one foot, or keep both if it is too hard.
4. Slowly lower your heel below the step over about three seconds.
5. Use both feet to come back up to the top.
6. Repeat, then switch sides if you are doing one leg.

**Tips:** Go slowly on the way down and don't bounce. Mild aching is normal, but stop if you feel sharp pain. Do it with a straight knee, and with a slightly bent knee on other sets to reach the deeper calf muscle.

### In daily life
Your calves do this every time you walk down stairs or lower yourself off a curb.

### Why it matters
Runners, court and field players, gymnasts, climbers and dancers load the Achilles tendon with every push-off and landing. Slow lowering builds its strength and is widely used both to treat sore Achilles tendons and to help prevent them.
