An interval run is a set of hard runs at a fixed distance, each followed by rest. It raises speed and aerobic power in a way a steady run cannot.

### How to do it
1. Mark the distance on a track, road or path, for example 200 m, 400 m or 800 m.
2. Warm up with 10 to 15 minutes of easy running and a few strides.
3. Run the first repeat hard but steady, at the same pace you can hold for all of them.
4. Walk or jog for the rest period.
5. Repeat. A common set is 8 x 200 m with 90 seconds rest, or 6 x 400 m with 2 minutes rest.
6. Cool down with an easy jog.

**Tips:** Hold an even pace and don't go too fast on the first one. Keep your shoulders loose. Cut a rep if your form falls apart.

### In daily life
The fitness helps you run for a train or keep up with the children.

### Why it matters
Runners use intervals to train for races, and field players use them to repeat sprints. They raise the top pace you can sustain.
