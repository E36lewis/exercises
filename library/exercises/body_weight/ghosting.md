Ghosting is racket footwork done without a ball. You move from the court center to set corners and back as if playing the shots against a ghost opponent.

### How to do it
1. Mark the center and four or six corners of the court with cones, about 3 to 4 meters (3 to 4 yards) from the middle.
2. Stand in the ready position at the center, with a split step.
3. Move to a corner in a call-out or at random, swinging as if hitting the shot.
4. Push back to the center.
5. Work 20 to 40 seconds with 20 to 30 seconds rest for 6 to 8 rounds.

**Tips:** Stay low in the ready position. Take a split step before each move. Recover the center fully between shots.

### In daily life
Short bursts of side and diagonal steps are used when playing with children or dodging obstacles.

### Why it matters
Badminton, squash, tennis and table tennis players use it to build the stamina and footwork for rallies.
