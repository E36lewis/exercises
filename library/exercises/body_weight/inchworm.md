The inchworm is a dynamic warm-up that walks your hands out from a standing fold into a plank and back. It stretches the hamstrings and calves, and wakes up the shoulders and trunk.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet hip-width apart.
2. Bend forward and place your hands on the floor, bending your knees if you need to.
3. Walk your hands forward until you are in a straight-arm plank.
4. Hold for a second, with your trunk braced.
5. Walk your feet toward your hands, keeping your legs as straight as you can.
6. Stand up and repeat for 6 to 10 reps.

**Tips:** Brace your trunk so your hips do not sag in the plank. Take small steps with your feet. Bend the knees if your hamstrings pull your back round.

### In daily life
It is the movement of reaching for something on the floor and getting back up.

### Why it matters
It's a quick whole-body warm-up for any sport. It opens the back of the legs and loads the shoulders and trunk for crawling, pushing and running.
