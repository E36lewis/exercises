The airborne heisman is a body-weight cardio drill in which you leap forward off one leg, drive the opposite knee up and push the arm on the other side out in front, like a ball carrier fending off a tackler. It works the thighs, calves and hip flexors.

### How to do it
1. Stand tall with your feet together and your arms bent at your sides.
2. Step forward with your left foot and push off it with force.
3. As you rise, drive your right knee up toward hip height.
4. At the same time, extend your left arm out in front with the palm open, as if holding someone back.
5. Land softly on the left foot, bending the knee to absorb the impact.
6. Repeat on the other side, alternating legs as you travel forward.

**Tips:** Stay tall and let your arms swing against your legs. Push up and forward rather than lunging. Keep landings soft and your knees tracking over your toes. Make sure you have a clear stretch of floor or grass ahead of you.

### In daily life
The long, springy stride is the same bounding pattern you use to leap over a puddle or hurry across a stream on stepping stones.

### Why it matters
Bounding off one leg can help build explosive leg power and rhythm, and landing on one foot at a time supports single-leg control, which is useful in running and field sports.
