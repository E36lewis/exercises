Standing on a step on one leg, you lower the other heel toward the floor by bending the standing knee. It trains the thigh and hip to stop the knee from collapsing inward.

### How to do it
1. Stand on the edge of a step, on your right leg, with your left foot hanging off the side.
2. Keep your hips level and your chest tall.
3. Bend your right knee and slowly lower your left heel toward the floor.
4. Keep your right knee over your toes, not drifting inward.
5. Tap the floor lightly with your heel.
6. Push through your right foot to stand back up. Finish the reps, then switch.

**Tips:** Go slowly, in about three seconds down. Use a low step at first. If the knee caves in or the hip drops, use a lower step or hold a rail.

### In daily life
It is the same control you use stepping off a curb or walking down stairs without your knee wobbling.

### Why it matters
Running, court, field, skating and snow sports involve landing and cutting on one leg, where the knee can collapse inward. This drill trains the control used in knee and ACL injury prevention programs.
