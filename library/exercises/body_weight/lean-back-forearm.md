A stretch for the forearm and wrist, using your own body weight. With your palms flat and fingers pointing back, you lean away to lengthen the muscles on the palm side of your forearm that bend the wrist and fingers.

### How to do it
1. Kneel on a mat with your hands flat on the floor in front of you.
2. Turn your hands so your fingers point back toward your knees, palms flat.
3. Straighten your elbows gently.
4. Slowly lean your hips back until you feel a stretch along your forearms.
5. Hold for a comfortable period, breathing steadily.
6. Come out slowly.

**Tips:** Lean only as far as you feel a mild stretch, never sharp pain. Keep your palms pressed flat. If your wrists are sore, start with your fingers pointing sideways, or skip it.

### In daily life
It eases the tight, tired feeling in your forearms after typing, using a phone, gripping a steering wheel or carrying bags.

### Why it matters
A more mobile wrist and forearm can help with grip comfort and with positions that load the wrists, such as push-ups and front rack holds.
