An exaggerated running stride in which you leap from one foot to the other, covering as much ground as you can. It builds running power and stride length.

### How to do it
1. Start with a short jog to get moving.
2. Push off one foot and drive the opposite knee up and forward.
3. Spring forward, reaching with the lead leg and swinging your opposite arm.
4. Land on the opposite foot and immediately push off again.
5. Keep going for 10 to 20 metres with a long flight in each stride.
6. Walk back to recover.

**Tips:** Warm up well first. Land on the ball of your foot under your hips, with a springy landing. Stop when you tire and your landings get heavy. Use soft grass or a track.

### In daily life
It's an exaggerated version of the push off you use to leap over a stream.

### Why it matters
Running, field, snow and skating sports depend on how far and how fast you can push off with each stride. Bounding trains the power and stride length for sprinting and the push for skating and skiing.
