Diaphragmatic breathing, or belly breathing, uses the diaphragm to draw air deep into the lungs. It trains breath control and calms the nervous system.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back with your knees bent, or sit tall.
2. Place one hand on your chest and the other on your belly.
3. Breathe in slowly through your nose for about 4 seconds, letting the belly rise while the chest stays still.
4. Breathe out through pursed lips for about 6 seconds, feeling the belly fall.
5. Repeat for 5 to 10 minutes, or 10 breaths.

**Tips:** Keep the shoulders soft and the chest still. Go slowly and don't force the air. Stop if you feel dizzy.

### In daily life
Deep, slow breathing helps with stress, sleep and getting your heart rate down.

### Why it matters
Shooters, archers and other precision athletes use slow breathing to steady their aim and heart rate before a shot.
