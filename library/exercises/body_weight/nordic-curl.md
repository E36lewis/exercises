A bodyweight exercise where you kneel with your feet held down and lower your body forward, using your hamstrings (the back of your thighs) to slow the fall. It is a slow, controlled lowering, and the calves and hips help.

### How to do it
1. Kneel on a pad with your ankles held firmly under a bar or by a partner.
2. Keep your body straight from knees to head.
3. Cross your arms or hold your hands ready in front of you.
4. Lean forward slowly, using your hamstrings to control the fall.
5. Lower as far as you can control, then catch yourself with your hands.
6. Push off the floor and use your hamstrings to help return upright.

**Tips:** Keep your hips extended; don't bend at the waist. Go only as far as you can control. This is demanding, so start with a short range to avoid straining your hamstrings.

### In daily life
Your hamstrings do this kind of braking when you walk downhill or slow yourself as you sit down, and they work hard when you sprint.

### Why it matters
It can build hamstring strength, particularly in the lengthening phase, which supports sprinting and injury resilience.
