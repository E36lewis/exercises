The inverted row pulls your chest up to a fixed bar while you hang beneath it, with your heels on the floor. You use your own bodyweight as the load, so it works the back and the arms, and the body stays in a plank.

### How to do it
1. Set a bar at about waist height and lie on your back beneath it.
2. Grip the bar with your hands slightly wider than your shoulders.
3. Straighten your body from heels to head and keep your heels on the floor.
4. Pull your chest up toward the bar by drawing your elbows down and back.
5. Pause when your chest is near the bar.
6. Lower yourself slowly until your arms are straight.

**Tips:** Keep your hips up and don't let them sag. Bend your knees, or raise the bar, to make it easier. Lower the bar to make it harder.

### In daily life
Pulling yourself up from a lying position, or hauling yourself onto a ledge, calls on the same pulling muscles.

### Why it matters
It builds pulling strength from a position that is kinder than a pull-up, and it adjusts easily. That can help your posture and prepare you for pull-ups.
