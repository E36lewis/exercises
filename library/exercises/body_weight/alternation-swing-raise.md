The alternation swing raise is a body-weight movement in which you swing your arms forward and up one at a time, like a slow walking-pace arm swing. It works the front of the shoulders and warms up the upper body.

### How to do it
1. Stand tall with your feet shoulder-width apart and your arms by your sides.
2. Brace your abdominals lightly.
3. Swing your right arm forward and up to shoulder height or just above, keeping it nearly straight.
4. Lower it with control as you raise your left arm.
5. Keep alternating arms in a smooth rhythm.
6. After a set, shake your arms out.

**Tips:** Move from the shoulder and avoid leaning back. Keep your neck relaxed. Stay within a range that feels comfortable, and do not force your arm higher if you feel a pinch in the shoulder.

### In daily life
The forward-and-up reach is the same you use to take a jar from a shelf, hang washing on a line or wave to someone across a street.

### Why it matters
It can help warm up the shoulders before heavier work, and the slow rhythm can support control through the range you use for everyday reaching.
