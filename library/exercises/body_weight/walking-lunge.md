The walking lunge is a body-weight exercise in which you step forward into a lunge, then keep going by stepping through with the other leg. It mainly works the quadriceps (the vasti group), with the glutes, adductors and calves helping.

### How to do it
1. Stand tall with your feet together and your hands on your hips or by your sides.
2. Take a long step forward with your right foot.
3. Bend both knees and lower your back knee toward the floor, keeping your torso upright.
4. Stop when your front thigh is about parallel to the floor, with your front shin close to vertical.
5. Push through your front foot, bring your back foot forward and step into a lunge with the other leg.
6. Keep walking, alternating legs.

**Tips:** Take a step long enough that your front knee stays roughly above your ankle. Keep your front knee pointing in the same direction as your toes. Keep your chest up and your hips level. Find a clear stretch of floor.

### In daily life
It matches the leg effort of climbing stairs, hiking uphill or stepping over obstacles, where one leg carries your weight at a time.

### Why it matters
Working one leg at a time challenges balance and hip stability, and it shows up any strength difference between your two legs.
