The split step is a small, quick hop made just as your opponent hits the ball. You land with your feet wider than your hips and your weight on the balls of your feet, ready to push off in any direction.

### How to do it
1. Stand tall with your feet about hip-width apart and your knees soft.
2. Hop an inch or two off the floor, with the feet leaving together.
3. Land on the balls of both feet, wider than your shoulders, knees bent and weight low.
4. From that landing, push off at once toward a cone, a partner's call or a feint.
5. Reset and repeat. Program it as 4 to 6 sets of 20 to 30 seconds, or 10 to 12 reps with a move after each.

**Tips:** Keep the hop small and time the landing to the moment of the stimulus. Land quietly, with heels light. Do not let your knees cave in.

### In daily life
Catching your balance when a bus jolts or when you step off a kerb you did not see uses the same quick, ready landing.

### Why it matters
It is the ready hop before every shot in tennis, badminton, squash, volleyball and similar games. A well-timed split step loads the legs like a spring so the first step toward the ball is faster.
