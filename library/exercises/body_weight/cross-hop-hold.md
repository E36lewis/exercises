The cross hop hold is a bodyweight drill in which you hop across a line and hold the landing on one leg. The hold is the point: it trains the glutes, thighs, calves and hip muscles to stop you and keep you steady.

### How to do it
1. Stand on one leg next to a line on the floor, with a slight bend in your knee.
2. Swing your arms back and bend into your hip.
3. Hop sideways or diagonally across the line, swinging your arms forward.
4. Land on the opposite foot, bending your knee and hip to absorb the landing.
5. Hold the landing still for a few seconds, chest up and knee in line with your toes.
6. Reset, hop back, and switch legs.

**Tips:** Hold only as long as you can stay steady. Keep your knee from caving in. Use a non-slip floor, and stop if your landings become wobbly. Try 3 to 5 sets of 6 to 10 hops each way, holding each landing for 2 to 3 seconds, with 60 seconds of rest.

### In daily life
It trains the balance you need to stop suddenly on a trail or step off a moving bus without losing your footing.

### Why it matters
It can help build single-leg stability and braking control, which supports knee and ankle resilience.
