From a kneeling position you lean slowly backward, keeping your body straight from knees to head, then return. It loads the quadriceps and the patellar tendon while they are stretched.

### How to do it
1. Kneel on a soft mat with your knees hip-width apart and your body upright.
2. Cross your arms over your chest or hold them forward for balance.
3. Squeeze your glutes and brace your abdomen.
4. Lean back slowly, keeping a straight line from knees to head.
5. Go as far as you can control, then use your thighs to pull back up.
6. Repeat.

**Tips:** Keep your hips forward and don't fold at the waist. Start with a small lean. Pad your knees, and stop if you feel knee pain rather than a strong pull in the thigh. Hold a post at first if needed.

### In daily life
It trains the thighs the way they work when you lower yourself down to kneel to garden or pick up a toy.

### Why it matters
Snow, court, running, field and skating sports load the front of the knee with jumps, landings and downhill running. Strengthening the quadriceps in a lengthened position builds the tendon and helps prevent kneecap tendon problems.
