The lateral shuffle is a side-to-side movement in a low athletic stance, without crossing the feet. It trains the hips and thighs that push you sideways and keeps you balanced while you move.

### How to do it
1. Place two cones 5 meters (about 5 yards) apart.
2. Start in a low stance, knees bent, chest up, feet wider than your shoulders.
3. Push off the trailing foot and step the lead foot sideways, then bring the trailing foot back without letting the feet touch.
4. Stay low and keep your toes forward.
5. Touch the cone with the outside hand, then shuffle back.
6. Work 20 to 30 seconds per round for 4 to 6 rounds, or 6 trips of 5 meters each way.

**Tips:** Keep the hips low and the head level. Do not cross or click your feet. Switch which side leads so both legs are trained.

### In daily life
Moving along a shelf, or sidestepping through a crowd, uses a small version of this step.

### Why it matters
Basketball, tennis, football, soccer and combat sports all call for sideways movement while facing the action. The drill builds the leg strength and balance to defend and cover the court.
