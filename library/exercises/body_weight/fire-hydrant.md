From hands and knees you lift one bent knee out to the side, like a dog at a fire hydrant. It builds endurance in the glute medius, the muscle on the outside of the hip.

### How to do it
1. Start on your hands and knees with your hands under your shoulders and your knees under your hips.
2. Brace your abdomen and keep your back flat.
3. Lift your right knee out to the side, keeping it bent to 90 degrees.
4. Stop when your thigh is about level with your hip, without rotating your pelvis.
5. Lower slowly.
6. Repeat, then switch sides.

**Tips:** Keep your hips square; don't roll your torso open. Move slowly. Only lift as high as you can without shifting your back.

### In daily life
The outer hip keeps your pelvis level when you walk, climb stairs or stand on one leg.

### Why it matters
Cyclists, runners and skaters push repeatedly for long periods and need hip muscles that don't tire. Strong outer hip muscles help keep the knee in line.
