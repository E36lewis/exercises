A balance drill where you stand on one leg and reach the other foot as far as you can in different directions. It trains the ankle, knee and hip to work together to keep you steady, like the star excursion balance test.

### How to do it
1. Stand on your right leg with a soft bend in the knee.
2. Reach your left foot forward along the floor as far as you can without putting weight on it.
3. Return to the middle without touching down.
4. Reach out to the side, then behind you, in the same way.
5. Keep your standing foot flat and your hips level.
6. Finish all reaches, then switch legs.

**Tips:** Keep your standing heel down and your knee over your toes, not caving in. Reach only as far as you can control. Hold a wall or chair lightly if you need help.

### In daily life
It trains the balance you use to step over a puddle, put on trousers standing up, or catch yourself on uneven ground.

### Why it matters
Every sport that involves cutting, landing or changing direction asks the ankle to stay steady on one foot. Better single-leg control helps with balance and ankle sprain prevention for everyone.
