The 180 switch jump is a body-weight jump in which you turn half a circle in the air and land facing the other way with your feet swapped. It is a cardio drill that works the thighs, hips and calves and raises your heart rate quickly.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet about hip-width apart and one foot slightly ahead of the other, knees soft.
2. Bend your knees and hips into a quarter squat and swing your arms back.
3. Jump up, driving your arms forward and up, and turn your body 180 degrees in the air.
4. Land facing the opposite direction with the other foot now forward.
5. Absorb the landing on the balls of your feet and sink into a quarter squat.
6. Pause to steady yourself, then jump back the other way. Keep going, alternating the direction of the turn.

**Tips:** Keep your chest up and your knees tracking over your toes. Land quietly; a loud landing means you are stiff. Start with small jumps on a non-slip surface, and ease off if your knees or ankles feel unsteady.

### In daily life
The turn-and-land pattern is what you do when you pivot quickly on the spot, such as spinning to catch a closing door or stepping out of the way of a cyclist.

### Why it matters
Jumping and turning together can help build leg power and coordination. Practising controlled landings from a rotation can support balance and healthy joints in fast sports such as basketball, tennis and football.
