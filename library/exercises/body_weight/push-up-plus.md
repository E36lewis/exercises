A push-up where you keep pushing at the top so your shoulder blades spread apart around your ribs. It trains the serratus anterior, which holds the shoulder blade against the rib cage.

### How to do it
1. Start in a push-up position with your hands under your shoulders.
2. Lower your chest toward the floor as in a normal push-up.
3. Push up until your arms are straight.
4. Keep pushing the floor away so that your upper back rounds slightly and your shoulder blades spread apart.
5. Pause for a moment.
6. Let your shoulder blades slide back together and repeat.

**Tips:** Keep your arms straight on the plus; the movement is in the shoulder blades only. Don't sag at the hips. Do it on your knees or against a wall if needed.

### In daily life
It is the push you use to shove a heavy door or lean on a counter to push yourself up.

### Why it matters
Bat, racket, water and collision sports involve throwing, swinging, swimming and blocking, which all depend on a steady shoulder blade. The serratus anterior keeps it in place and protects the shoulder.
