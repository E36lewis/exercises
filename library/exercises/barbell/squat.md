The barbell back squat is the classic loaded lower-body lift. The bar sits on your upper back as you bend your hips and knees. The thighs and glutes lift the load, and your trunk muscles keep you upright.

### How to do it
1. Set a barbell on a rack at about upper-chest height and step under it.
2. Rest the bar across the muscles of your upper back, not on your neck, and grip it just wider than your shoulders.
3. Lift the bar off the rack, step back and set your feet about shoulder-width apart, toes slightly out.
4. Take a breath, brace your core, then bend your hips and knees together to lower under control.
5. Go down until your thighs are about parallel to the floor, or as deep as you can with good form.
6. Push your whole foot into the floor to stand back up, then rack the bar.

**Tips:** Keep your heels down and your knees tracking in line with your toes. Do not let your lower back round at the bottom. Set the rack's safety bars just below your lowest position so they catch the bar if you fail.

### In daily life
This is the lift behind getting up from a low sofa or the toilet, and behind hoisting a heavy box off the floor.

### Why it matters
Barbell squats let you add load gradually, so they can build thigh and hip strength that supports your knees and balance.
