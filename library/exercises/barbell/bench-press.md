The barbell bench press is a classic strength lift done lying on a flat bench. The chest (pectoralis major) does most of the work, with help from the front of the shoulders and the triceps.

### How to do it
1. Lie on a flat bench with your eyes under the bar and your feet flat on the floor.
2. Pull your shoulder blades together and down, and keep them there.
3. Grip the bar a little wider than your shoulders and lift it off the rack so it sits over your chest.
4. Lower the bar under control to your mid-chest, with your elbows at about 45 degrees to your body.
5. Pause briefly, then press the bar back up until your arms are straight.
6. Re-rack the bar before you let go.

**Tips:** Keep your wrists stacked over your elbows and your feet planted. Do not bounce the bar off your chest. Wrap your thumbs around the bar, and use safety arms or a spotter.

### In daily life
It trains the push you use to shove a heavy door open or press yourself up from the ground.

### Why it matters
It can help build upper-body pushing strength, and the lowering phase teaches you to control a load around the shoulder joint.
