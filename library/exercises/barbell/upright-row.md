The upright row pulls a weight straight up the front of your body, leading with the elbows. It works the side of the shoulders (deltoids) and the upper trapezius, which sits between your neck and shoulders, with the biceps helping.

### How to do it
1. Stand tall with your feet hip-width apart, holding a bar or two dumbbells in front of your thighs with an overhand grip, hands about shoulder-width apart.
2. Brace your abs and keep your shoulders down.
3. Lead with your elbows and raise the weight along your body toward your chest.
4. Stop when your elbows are about level with your shoulders.
5. Lower the weight slowly back to the start.

**Tips:** Keep the weight close to your body and your wrists in line with your forearms. Do not lift your elbows much higher than your shoulders, because that can pinch the shoulder. Use a lighter weight and a slightly wider grip if your shoulders feel tight.

### In daily life
It trains the pull you use to lift something from a low position to chest height, such as raising a bag of groceries or a bucket from the floor to a counter.

### Why it matters
It builds the shoulder caps and the muscles that raise the shoulder blades, and it teaches you to lift with the elbows leading while the torso stays upright.
