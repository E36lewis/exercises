A barbell row with a wide grip and elbows flared out to the sides. Wide elbows shift the work to the rear of the shoulders (rear deltoids) and the upper back, with the biceps helping.

### How to do it
1. Stand holding a barbell and hinge forward at the hips with a flat back.
2. Take a grip wider than your shoulders.
3. Brace your abdomen and let the bar hang below your chest.
4. Pull the bar toward your lower chest, with your elbows pointing out.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top.
6. Lower the bar slowly.

**Tips:** Keep your back flat and don't jerk the bar. Use a lighter weight than for a normal row. Stop if you feel your lower back strain.

### In daily life
Pulling your arms back and out, as when you open a heavy double door or pull a rowing oar, uses these muscles.

### Why it matters
We spend much of the day with the arms in front of us. Training the rear shoulders can support posture and balance the strength of the front and back of the shoulder.
