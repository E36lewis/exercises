The barbell good morning is a hip-hinge exercise. With a bar on your upper back, you bend forward from the hips and stand back up. It loads the hamstrings and the muscles along your spine through a long range of motion.

### How to do it
1. Set a barbell on a rack at upper-chest height and step under it, resting it across your upper back as in a squat.
2. Lift it off the rack, step back and stand with feet about shoulder-width apart and knees slightly bent.
3. Brace your core, push your hips straight back and lean your chest forward with your back flat.
4. Lower until your torso is near parallel to the floor, or as far as you can keep your back flat.
5. Drive your hips forward to stand tall again, then rack the bar.

**Tips:** Keep the movement a hinge, not a squat, and keep your back flat from head to tailbone. Start with an empty bar. Use a rack with safety bars set at your lowest bar position, because this lift can go wrong fast if your back rounds.

### In daily life
It is the same hinge you use to pick up a bag from the floor or to lean over a sink without rounding your back.

### Why it matters
Running, field, collision, wheel and combat sports demand strong hamstrings and a strong lower back. The long range in this lift builds both and teaches you to hinge safely.
