This is a bent-over pulling exercise with a barbell. You hold a hinged position and row the bar towards your lower ribs. It mainly works the lats, with the upper back, rear shoulders and arms helping, while the legs and lower back hold you in place.

### How to do it
1. Hold a barbell with an overhand grip, slightly wider than shoulder-width.
2. Hinge forward at the hips with soft knees, back flat and chest up, bar hanging below your shoulders.
3. Brace your core.
4. Pull the bar towards your lower ribs, driving your elbows back.
5. Pause, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
6. Lower the bar slowly until your arms are straight.

**Tips:** Keep your torso angle steady instead of standing up as you pull. Don't round your back. Use a weight you can row without swinging.

### In daily life
Dragging a heavy suitcase off a luggage carousel or pulling a bag of cement from a trolley uses the same pull from a hinged stance.

### Why it matters
It builds a strong back and supports good posture. It also teaches your back and legs to hold a hinged position under load, which carries over to deadlifts and other lifts.
