Bike intervals alternate hard efforts with easy recovery on a stationary bike or a road bike. The legs push in circles, and there is no impact, so the joints are spared. It is a flexible way to build heart and lung fitness.

### How to do it
1. Set the seat so your knee is slightly bent at the bottom of the pedal stroke, and the handlebars so you can sit tall without strain.
2. Warm up with 5 to 10 minutes of easy pedalling.
3. Ride hard for the work period, for example 30 seconds, at an effort you can only keep up for that long.
4. Ease off to a light spin for the recovery, for example 90 seconds, and keep your legs moving.
5. Repeat for 6 to 10 rounds.
6. Cool down with 5 minutes of easy spinning.

**Tips:** Common formats are 10 x 30 s on, 90 s off for short efforts, or 4 x 4 min hard with 3 min easy for longer ones. Keep the last effort as strong as the first. Hold the handlebars loosely and keep your hips steady on the seat.

### In daily life
Cycling to work, up a hill or against a headwind uses the same pushing pattern, and the hard-then-easy rhythm is like real traffic and terrain.

### Why it matters
Every sport benefits from a bigger aerobic engine and quicker recovery between efforts. Because the bike has no impact, it suits athletes in running, field, court and collision sports who need conditioning without extra pounding on the knees, hips and feet, and it is a good way to train around a sore joint.
