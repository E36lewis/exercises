A bike sprint is a short, all-out effort on a bike, usually 6 to 30 seconds, followed by a long rest. It builds top-end power with no impact on the joints.

### How to do it
1. Set up your bike or stationary bike with the seat at hip height when standing next to it.
2. Warm up with 5 to 10 minutes of easy riding and a couple of short builds.
3. Start from a slow roll in an easy gear, or from a standstill.
4. Sprint all-out, pedalling hard and keeping your upper body steady.
5. Keep the effort going to the end of the set time, then ease off and spin lightly.
6. Rest fully before the next sprint.

**Tips:** A usual plan is 6 to 10 sprints of 10 to 20 seconds with 2 to 3 minutes' easy spinning between. Keep your hands firmly on the bars. On a road bike use a quiet, straight road and watch for traffic. Stop when your power drops noticeably.

### In daily life
Racing to cross at a light, pedalling hard up a short hill and sprinting after a bus all use a quick burst of leg power.

### Why it matters
Wheel sports such as cycling need big bursts of power, and skate and field sports need repeated sprints. The bike lets athletes train max efforts without the pounding of running.
