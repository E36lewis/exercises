The hurdle hop is a series of two-footed jumps over low hurdles. Each landing flows straight into the next jump. It builds quick, reactive power.

### How to do it
1. Set 4 to 6 low hurdles, 30 to 50 cm high, in a line about 1 metre apart.
2. Stand facing the first hurdle with your feet hip-width apart.
3. Swing your arms, jump and bring your knees up to clear the hurdle.
4. Land softly on the balls of your feet and jump again right away.
5. Keep your contact time on the ground short.
6. Walk back and repeat.

**Tips:** Start with lower hurdles and a slower pace. Do 3 to 5 sets of one run, with 1 to 2 minutes' rest. Use a soft surface such as turf or a mat, and hurdles that tip over if you catch them. Stop when the jumps become sloppy.

### In daily life
Hopping over puddles, jumping across a creek and clearing a low fence need the same quick two-footed jump.

### Why it matters
Running, field and court sports all use quick, springy movement. Hurdle hops build reactive power, which helps sprinting speed and jumping.
