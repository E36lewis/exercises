The hurdle walkover is a dynamic warm-up in which you walk sideways or forward over low hurdles, lifting each leg high and rotating at the hip. It opens up the hips and gets them ready to run.

### How to do it
1. Set 4 to 6 hurdles, 40 to 60 cm high, in a line 1 metre apart.
2. Stand sideways to the first hurdle.
3. Lift your lead leg, knee first, up and over the hurdle, then bring the trail leg over in the same circle.
4. Place your foot down on the other side and step on to the next hurdle.
5. Keep your trunk tall and move slowly.
6. After one trip, turn around and lead with the other leg.

**Tips:** Do 2 or 3 trips each way before sprints or jumps. Keep your hips level and move in a smooth circle. Use lower hurdles at first. Make sure that hurdles are stable and you step clear of them.

### In daily life
Stepping over a fence, getting in and out of a car and clearing a low obstacle on a trail use the same hip circle.

### Why it matters
Runners and field athletes need free, powerful hip movement to run fast. The walkover warms up the hips with movement in many directions, which helps stride and reduces stiffness before training.
