A shuttle run is a repeated sprint between two lines or cones. You run to one line, touch it, turn and run back, over and over. It builds speed endurance and the ability to stop and start.

### How to do it
1. Place two cones 10 to 20 m apart.
2. Start at one cone in a staggered stance.
3. Sprint to the other cone and touch the ground or the line.
4. Turn quickly, driving off the outside foot, and sprint back.
5. Keep going for the set number of lengths or the set time.
6. Rest fully, then repeat.

**Tips:** Common sets are 6 x 20 m with 30 seconds' rest, or 4 to 6 rounds of 30 seconds on and 30 seconds off. Slow down into each turn rather than skidding, and keep your hips low. Use non-slip footwear and a clear, flat surface.

### In daily life
Running back and forth to fetch things, chasing a ball and catching a bus after you've turned around all use short sprints and turns.

### Why it matters
Nearly all sports are made of repeated sprints with short rests. Shuttle runs build that capacity, which is why they are such a common conditioning tool.
