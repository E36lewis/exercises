The L drill, also known as the three-cone drill, has you sprint, backpedal and weave around three cones set in an L. It tests and trains acceleration and tight changes of direction.

### How to do it
1. Place three cones in an L shape, each 5 yards (about 4.5 m) apart: one at the corner and one at each end.
2. Start in a stance at the first cone.
3. Sprint to the corner cone, touch the line, and run back to the start.
4. Turn and sprint to the corner again, then run around the inside of the third cone.
5. Weave around the corner cone and sprint back to the finish, keeping your inside shoulder low.
6. Walk back and rest before the next run.

**Tips:** Do 4 to 6 runs with 1 to 2 minutes' rest. Plant on the outside foot and drive out of every turn. Keep your hips low and your eyes ahead. Use a dry, grippy surface and warm up well, because the cuts are hard on the knees and ankles.

### In daily life
Jumping to avoid an obstacle, cutting around a corner and weaving through a busy area need quick changes of direction.

### Why it matters
Field and collision sports are made of short bursts and tight cuts. The L drill is the standard test of that and a good way to practise it.
