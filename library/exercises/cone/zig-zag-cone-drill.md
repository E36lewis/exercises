The zig zag cone drill has you run around a line of offset cones, cutting sharply at about 45 degrees at each. It trains repeated changes of direction at speed.

### How to do it
1. Place 5 cones in a zig zag pattern, each about 5 yards (about 4.5 m) apart and offset 3 yards (about 2.7 m) from the line.
2. Start at the first cone.
3. Sprint at an angle to the second cone and plant your outside foot.
4. Cut sharply toward the third cone, driving off the planted foot.
5. Keep zig-zagging to the end.
6. Jog back and repeat.

**Tips:** Do 4 to 6 runs with 1 to 2 minutes' rest. Stay low during the cut, with your hips and eyes ahead. Plant the outside foot beyond the cone and push away. Use a grippy surface. Warm up first, because of the load on the knees.

### In daily life
Weaving through traffic on foot, dodging objects while running and playing with children use the same cuts.

### Why it matters
Field and racket sports are made of cuts and quick changes of direction. This drill gives repeated practice at the angle where most cuts happen.
