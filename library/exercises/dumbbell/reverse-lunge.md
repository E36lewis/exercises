A lunge where you step backward while holding a dumbbell in each hand. It works the thighs (quadriceps) and glutes, with the hamstrings and calves helping, and your core holds you upright.

### How to do it
1. Stand tall, holding dumbbells at your sides.
2. Step back with one foot, landing on the ball of that foot.
3. Lower your back knee toward the floor, with your torso upright.
4. Keep your front knee over your ankle.
5. Push through your front foot to return to standing.
6. Repeat on the other side, or finish all reps on one leg first.

**Tips:** Take a step long enough that your front shin stays almost vertical. Use a lighter weight than you would for a forward lunge. Stop if you feel knee pain.

### In daily life
Stepping back to make room, or lowering yourself to the floor with a box or basket in your hands, uses the same motion.

### Why it matters
Stepping back is often kinder on the front knee than a forward lunge, and it trains one leg at a time, which can help even out strength between sides.
