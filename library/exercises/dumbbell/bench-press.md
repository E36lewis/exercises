The dumbbell bench press is a pressing exercise done lying on a flat bench. It works the chest (pectoralis major), the front of the shoulders and the triceps. Dumbbells let each arm move on its own path, which also asks more of your stabilizing muscles.

### How to do it
1. Sit on the end of a flat bench with a dumbbell on each thigh. Lie back, bringing the dumbbells to chest level.
2. Plant your feet flat on the floor and keep your shoulder blades pulled back and down.
3. Hold the dumbbells over your chest with your palms facing forward.
4. Press them up until your arms are nearly straight.
5. Lower them slowly with your elbows at about 45 degrees to your body.
6. Stop when the weights reach chest level and press again.

**Tips:** Keep your wrists straight and your lower back with a natural curve. Do not bounce the weights. Use a weight you can control, and ask for a spotter on heavy sets.

### In daily life
Pressing forward and up is the same effort as pushing a stuck cart or heavy door, or lifting a child from a lying position.

### Why it matters
It is a basic way to build upper-body pushing strength. Because each arm works independently, a stronger side cannot take over, which can help keep your left and right sides even.
