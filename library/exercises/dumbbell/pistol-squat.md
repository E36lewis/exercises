A single-leg squat where you hold a dumbbell in front of you to balance the free leg. It works the front of the thigh (quadriceps) and the calves, and it tests balance and ankle and hip mobility.

### How to do it
1. Hold one dumbbell with both hands at your chest and stand on one leg, the other leg lifted in front of you.
2. Brace your abdomen and look ahead.
3. Sit back and down on the standing leg, keeping your free leg off the floor.
4. Go as low as you can while your heel stays flat and your knee follows the line of your toes.
5. Pause briefly at the bottom.
6. Press through the whole foot to stand, then switch legs.

**Tips:** Hold a doorframe or sit to a box first. The dumbbell works best as a counterweight, so start with a light one. Stop if you feel knee or ankle pain.

### In daily life
Standing up from a low seat on one leg, or lowering yourself to pick something up while carrying a bag on the other side, uses the same one-leg control.

### Why it matters
Most of your steps are taken on one leg at a time. Training each leg alone can help you spot and even out side-to-side differences in strength and balance.
