This is a hip-hinge lift with a dumbbell in each hand. You lower the weights by pushing your hips back, with your knees only slightly bent. It mainly works the hamstrings, glutes and the muscles along your spine, with your calves and thighs helping.

### How to do it
1. Stand tall with your feet about hip-width apart, holding the dumbbells in front of your thighs.
2. Soften your knees slightly and brace your core.
3. Push your hips back as if closing a door behind you, and let the dumbbells slide down your thighs.
4. Keep your back flat and your shoulders pulled back.
5. Lower until you feel a strong stretch in the back of your thighs, usually around mid-shin.
6. Drive your hips forward and squeeze your glutes to stand tall.

**Tips:** Keep the weights close to your legs. Don't round your lower back, and don't let your knees bend into a squat. Choose a weight you can control on the way down.

### In daily life
This is the pattern for picking a laundry basket off the floor or lifting a toddler without bending your back. It also helps when you straighten up after tying a shoe.

### Why it matters
It builds strength in the back of the legs and hips, and it teaches a safe hinge. A strong hinge supports a stable back when you lift, and it can help with running and jumping.
