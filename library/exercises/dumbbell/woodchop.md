The dumbbell woodchop is a rotational core exercise in which you move a dumbbell diagonally across your body in a chopping motion. It mainly works the obliques, with the spinal erectors, lats and glutes helping to power and steady the turn.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your feet slightly wider than your hips, holding a dumbbell with both hands beside one hip.
2. Bend your knees slightly and brace your abs.
3. Lift the dumbbell diagonally up across your body to above the opposite shoulder, turning your trunk and pivoting your back foot.
4. Keep your arms mostly straight and follow the weight with your eyes.
5. Lower it slowly along the same path to the start.
6. Finish your set, then switch to the other side.

**Tips:** Turn from your trunk and hips rather than only swinging your arms. Keep your hips facing forward more than your shoulders. Use a weight light enough that you can control the way back down.

### In daily life
It resembles lifting a bag from the floor to a shelf on the opposite side, or lifting a child onto your hip.

### Why it matters
Moving the weight through a diagonal path trains the trunk to produce and resist rotation, which helps protect the lower back in twisting movements.
