The icky shuffle is a lateral footwork drill through an agility ladder. You step in with one foot, bring the other in, and then step out sideways, so each square gets quick contacts. It trains fast, controlled lateral steps.

### How to do it
1. Lay an agility ladder flat on the floor. A 4 to 5 m ladder with squares about 45 cm long works well.
2. Stand facing along the ladder, just beside the first square.
3. Step the near foot into the square, then the other foot into the same square.
4. Step the first foot out to the far side, and the other foot out behind it.
5. Repeat into the next square, moving diagonally along the ladder.
6. Stay on the balls of your feet and keep your arms moving.

**Tips:** Walk it first, then speed up. Do 3 to 5 passes with a walk back. For intervals, work for 15 to 20 seconds, then rest 40 seconds. Keep your eyes up, not on your feet, and keep contacts light and quiet. Make sure the floor is dry and flat.

### In daily life
Weaving between objects in a crowded hall, stepping sideways along a ledge and dodging a ball at a picnic use this pattern.

### Why it matters
Field and court sports demand quick lateral steps in tight spaces. The icky shuffle trains the nervous system to make fast, accurate contacts, which carries over to defending, cutting and changing direction.
