The sandbag carry is walking with a heavy sandbag held in front of you or on your shoulder. The bag shifts and is hard to grip, so your trunk and grip work harder than with a barbell or dumbbell.

### How to do it
1. Load a sandbag to a challenging but safe weight, for example 20 to 40 kg.
2. Lift it with a flat back and hug it to your chest, or place it on one shoulder.
3. Stand tall, brace your trunk and take short, steady steps.
4. Walk 20 to 40 m without letting the bag slip.
5. Set the bag down by bending your knees, not your back.
6. Rest, then carry again, switching sides if on one shoulder.

**Tips:** A usual plan is 3 to 5 carries of 20 to 40 m. Keep your ribs down and your hands locked together. Clear the route first. Don't drop the bag from height.

### In daily life
Carrying a bag of cement, a child or a heavy load of laundry uses the same hold and the same bracing.

### Why it matters
Collision and combat sports involve gripping and moving an awkward object, such as an opponent. Run athletes gain trunk stiffness. The sandbag shifts, so it develops strength that a balanced bar doesn't.
