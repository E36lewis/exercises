Sandbag shouldering lifts a heavy sandbag from the ground to your shoulder in one movement. It uses the hips and legs to drive the bag up. It copies lifting and throwing an opponent in wrestling or rugby.

### How to do it
1. Stand over a sandbag with your feet hip-width apart.
2. Squat down, hug the bag and keep your back flat.
3. Stand up, driving through your hips and legs, and pull the bag against your thighs.
4. Extend your hips and pull the bag up the front of your body.
5. Roll the bag onto one shoulder, bending your knees as you do.
6. Stand tall, then drop the bag in front of you, with control, and repeat on the other shoulder.

**Tips:** Use 3 to 5 sets of 4 to 6 reps per side with a moderate bag, such as 20 to 40 kg. Keep your back flat and your hips low. Use a mat or a soft surface so the bag can land. Clear the area and don't swing the bag toward other people.

### In daily life
Lifting a sack onto a shoulder, putting a bag into a truck bed and picking up a child to your shoulder use the same movement.

### Why it matters
Collision and combat sports involve lifting and moving heavy, shifting bodies. Shouldering builds the hip power and back strength for it.
