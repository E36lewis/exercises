The backward sled drag is a slow walk backward while pulling a loaded sled on a strap. The thigh muscles work each step to straighten the knee, with almost no impact or lowering load. It is a long-standing knee-health exercise.

### How to do it
1. Attach a harness or a strap to a sled and load it lightly to moderately. Start with a plate or two.
2. Stand facing the sled with the strap taut and your knees slightly bent.
3. Take a long step backward and push through the front of your foot, straightening your knee.
4. Keep your chest tall and your hips under you as you walk backward.
5. Walk 10 to 20 m, then turn around and rest.

**Tips:** Typical work is 4 to 6 drags of 15 to 30 m, with a short rest. Keep the steps smooth and don't lock your knees. Check behind you before starting, because you can't see where you are going. Keep the load low enough that your steps stay even.

### In daily life
Backing a trailer up a hill, walking backward while pulling a cart, or stepping back down a steep incline uses the same thigh drive.

### Why it matters
Snow, court, run, field and skate sports all ask a lot of the quadriceps and the knees. Backward dragging builds the thighs with little stress on the knee joint, and it is a prehab staple used to bring athletes back from knee pain and to protect them before a season.
