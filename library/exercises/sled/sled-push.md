The sled push drives a weighted sled across the floor with straight arms and a forward lean. The legs push the ground away, as in the first steps of a sprint. There is almost no lowering phase, so you get much less muscle soreness than from most leg work.

### How to do it
1. Load the sled, for example 1 to 1.5 times your body weight on a smooth floor or turf for a first heavy push. Start lighter if you are new.
2. Grip the high handles, or the low ones for a lower, harder push, with arms straight.
3. Lean forward in a straight line from your head to your heels.
4. Drive forward with powerful, steady steps, and push the ground behind you.
5. Push for 10 to 20 m, then stop and rest before the next trip.

**Tips:** A usual plan is 4 to 8 pushes of 10 to 20 m with 1 to 2 minutes' rest. Keep your back flat and don't let the sled push you upright. Keep the sled path clear of people, and don't let go of the sled while it is moving.

### In daily life
Pushing a stalled car, moving a heavy piece of furniture or driving through a crowd all use this lean-and-drive pattern.

### Why it matters
Acceleration is key for field, collision, court, run and wheel sports. The sled push builds strength in the first steps, when the foot is behind the body, with almost no eccentric load, so you recover quickly and can train it often, even in season.
