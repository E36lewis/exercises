The two-headed muscle that forms the rounded bulge of the calf. Both heads start on the thigh bone just above the knee, so the muscle crosses the knee as well as the ankle. It joins the soleus below to form the Achilles tendon, which attaches to the heel bone.

### What it does
- Points the foot down (plantar flexion), as when you rise onto your toes.
- Helps bend the knee, because it crosses that joint.
- Produces the most force when the knee is straight. Bending the knee slackens it, and the soleus takes more of the work.
- Absorbs and returns spring energy through the Achilles tendon in hops and strides.

### In daily life
Pushing off the ground to walk or run, standing on tiptoe to reach a high shelf, climbing stairs, and landing from a small jump.

### Why it matters
Calf strength can help the lower leg handle the repeated loads of running and jumping. It also supports balance on uneven ground.

### Exercises that train it
Calf raise, Calf wall, Calf hop, Jump squat, Running, Tire hop
