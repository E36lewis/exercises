A small, narrow muscle deep in the buttock, one of the six deep hip rotators. It starts on the sit bone (ischial tuberosity) and runs sideways along the lower edge of the obturator internus tendon to the greater trochanter, the bony knob on the outside of the upper thigh bone. It lies just above the quadratus femoris.

### What it does
- Turns the thigh outward (external rotation) at the hip.
- Holds the ball of the thigh bone snug in its socket.
- Can help move the thigh out to the side when the hip is bent.

### In daily life
Turning your foot outward as you pivot on one leg, swinging your legs out of a car seat, and keeping the hip steady as you step down from a kerb.

### Why it matters
These small muscles work as a group to keep the hip centred as bigger muscles move the leg. That can help with balance and smooth, controlled leg movement.

### Exercises that train it
Lateral walk, Step up, Reverse lunge, Single leg romanian deadlift, Clam, Russian twist
