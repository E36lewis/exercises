The largest and heaviest muscle of the buttock, and the most superficial of the glutes. It is a broad, thick sheet that starts on the back of the pelvis, the sacrum and the tailbone. Most of it ends in the iliotibial band, the tough strip down the outer thigh, and the rest attaches to the back of the thigh bone.

### What it does
- Extends the hip, driving the thigh backward.
- Turns the thigh outward (external rotation).
- Its upper fibres lift the leg out to the side; its lower fibres draw it back toward the midline.
- Steadies the pelvis and trunk over the leg when you stand.

### In daily life
Standing up from a low chair, climbing stairs or a hill, sprinting, jumping, and straightening your back after bending to lift something.

### Why it matters
It is the body's main hip-extending power source. Strong glutes share load with the lower back and knees, which can help protect them.

### Exercises that train it
Deadlift, Hip thrust, Glute bridge marches, Kettlebell swing, Bulgarian split squat, Step up
