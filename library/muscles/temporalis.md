A broad, fan-shaped chewing muscle on the side of the head, above and in front of the ear. It spreads from the temporal fossa and runs under the cheekbone to the coronoid process of the lower jaw.

### What it does
- Raises the jaw to close the mouth, using the front fibres
- Pulls the jaw back with the rear fibres
- Helps the jaw grind side to side while chewing

### In daily life
It works when you bite into an apple, chew a tough steak or clench your teeth while concentrating. You can feel it bulge at your temple if you clench.

### Why it matters
It is one of the main chewing muscles. Overuse from clenching or grinding can lead to tension at the temples, and relaxing the jaw can help. It is not trained with weights.
