A ring of skeletal muscle around the lower end of the anal canal. Unlike the internal sphincter beneath it, this one is under voluntary control: you can tighten it on purpose.

### What it does
- Closes the anal opening to hold in stool and gas.
- Squeezes harder on command, when you need to wait.
- Relaxes to let waste pass.

### In daily life
You use it to hold a bowel movement until a toilet is available, and it tightens reflexively when you cough, laugh or sneeze.

### Why it matters
Along with the pelvic floor muscles, it contributes to continence. Childbirth, surgery and ageing can all affect it, and pelvic floor training can help with control. The library lists the perineal nerve for it; the muscle is supplied mainly by the inferior rectal branch of the pudendal nerve.
