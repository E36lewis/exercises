A long muscle on the back of the forearm, beside the brachioradialis. It starts above the elbow on the lower part of the outer humerus and attaches to the base of the second metacarpal, behind the index finger.

### What it does
- Extends the wrist (bends the hand back).
- Tilts the wrist toward the thumb side (radial deviation).

### In daily life
It brings your hand up and back when you wave, brace your hand to open a heavy door, or lift a paint tin by the handle.

### Why it matters
Its attachment above the elbow also lets it help the elbow bend a little, and it works with the other wrist extensors to keep the wrist strong in a grip. It is supplied by the radial nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Wrist curl palm down, Finger extensions, Lean back forearm, Side to side forearm, Push up fingers, Hammer curl
