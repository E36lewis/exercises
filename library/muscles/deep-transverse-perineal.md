A thin band of muscle running across the deep perineum, from one sit bone to the other, in front of the anus. It is well described in men and is often weak or missing in women.

### What it does
- Steadies the perineal body, the central point where pelvic floor muscles meet.
- Helps support the urethra.

### In daily life
It works unnoticed as you stand, walk and cough, helping keep the base of the pelvis firm.

### Why it matters
It is a small part of the web of pelvic floor muscles that can help support the organs of the pelvis. It is supplied by the perineal branch of the pudendal nerve.
