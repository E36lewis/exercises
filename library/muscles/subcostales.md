Thin muscle slips on the inner surface of the back of the rib cage, mostly near the lower ribs. Each runs down and inward from one rib across to the second or third rib below. They are part of the deep chest wall, not the shoulder.

### What it does
- Draws neighbouring ribs together
- Helps pull the lower ribs down
- May assist breathing out, though their exact role is still debated

### In daily life
They are active in the background when you cough, sigh or breathe out hard after a quick run for the bus.

### Why it matters
They help the rib cage stay firm and move in step with the other intercostal muscles. They are small and are not trained alone, but good breathing mechanics support trunk stability and effort.
