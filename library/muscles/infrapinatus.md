A thick, triangular rotator cuff muscle on the back of the shoulder blade, filling the hollow below the blade's spine. Its tendon crosses the back of the shoulder joint and attaches to the greater tubercle, the bump near the top of the upper arm bone. It is one of the four rotator cuff muscles.

### What it does
- Turns the arm outward (external rotation).
- Pulls the ball of the upper arm into its socket and holds it there as you move.
- Helps draw the arm backward.

### In daily life
Reaching behind you to buckle a seat belt, throwing a ball, swinging a racquet, and stretching an arm back for a coat sleeve.

### Why it matters
It is a main stabiliser of the shoulder and counters the pull of the chest muscles. Keeping it conditioned can help the shoulder move smoothly and stay balanced when you press and pull.

### Exercises that train it
Rotatory cuff external rotation, Banded pull apart, Arm circles, Wide chest, Single arm lateral raise, Turkish get up
