A ring of skeletal muscle around the urethra, the tube that carries urine out of the bladder. It sits in the pelvic floor and, unlike the inner sphincter at the bladder neck, it is under conscious control.

### What it does
- Squeezes the urethra shut to hold in urine.
- Tightens quickly and reflexively when you cough, sneeze or lift something.
- Relaxes so you can start to pass urine.

### In daily life
It lets you wait until you reach a toilet and stops leaks when you jump, laugh or cough.

### Why it matters
It is a key part of bladder control. Pelvic floor exercises (such as Kegels) can help to strengthen it, and it often weakens after childbirth or surgery. It is supplied by the perineal nerve, a branch of the pudendal nerve.
