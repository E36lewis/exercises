The large, rope-like muscle you can see and feel on each side of the neck. It runs from the breastbone and inner collarbone up and back to the bump behind the ear (the mastoid process).

### What it does
- Turns the head to the opposite side when one side works
- Tilts the head toward the same side
- Flexes the neck (tucks the chin) when both sides work
- Pulls the head forward on the neck, and can help lift the chest in deep breaths

### In daily life
It turns your head toward someone speaking beside you, lifts your head off a pillow when you get out of bed and helps you breathe in when you are out of breath after climbing stairs.

### Why it matters
It is the main neck flexor and a key head-turner, so it supports head control and posture. It can get tight with a forward head position, and balanced neck work can help keep it comfortable.
