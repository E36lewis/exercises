A thin, narrow muscle in the perineum, the area between the genitals and the anus. It runs sideways from the sit bone (ischial ramus) to the perineal body, a small tendon at the centre of the pelvic floor. It is often absent or very thin on one side.

### What it does
- Holds the perineal body in place
- Helps stabilise the centre of the pelvic floor
- Works with the muscles beside it to support the pelvic organs

### In daily life
It gives background support when you strain on the toilet, lift a heavy box or push during childbirth, as the pelvic floor takes the load.

### Why it matters
It is one of several small muscles that anchor the perineal body, so it contributes to pelvic floor strength. It is not trained on its own, but pelvic floor exercises work it with its neighbours.
