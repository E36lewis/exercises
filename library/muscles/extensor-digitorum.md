The main finger-straightening muscle, a flat muscle on the back of the forearm. It splits into four tendons that cross the back of the hand to the index, middle, ring and little fingers. Small bands link the tendons, so the fingers do not move completely independently.

### What it does
- Straightens (extends) the fingers at the knuckles.
- Helps extend the wrist, mostly when the fingers are held straight.
- Opens the hand wide after you let go of a grip.

### In daily life
It opens your hand to release a bar, reach for a door handle or spread your fingers to catch a ball.

### Why it matters
It is the opposite partner of the strong finger flexors. Training both can help keep the forearm balanced, and this muscle is worth attention if you spend long periods gripping or typing. It is supplied by the posterior interosseous nerve, the deep branch of the radial nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Finger extensions, Wrist curl palm down, Lean back forearm, Push up fingers, Side to side forearm
