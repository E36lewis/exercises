A set of short, deep muscle bundles that lie in the groove beside the spine, from the sacrum up to the neck. Each bundle crosses two to four vertebrae, running from the lower one up and inward to the spiny process of the vertebra above.

### What it does
- Stabilises each small segment of the spine.
- Extends the spine slightly (a back-arch motion).
- Bends the spine to the same side.
- Turns the trunk towards the opposite side.

### In daily life
Standing tall, lifting a bag from the floor, reaching across a table and stepping while keeping your trunk steady.

### Why it matters
It is a core stabiliser that keeps the spine controlled before larger muscles move it. A strong, well-coordinated multifidus can help the lower back handle everyday loads.

### Exercises that train it
Band plank march, Stability ball front plank, Turkish get up, Single arm bent over press, Circle run
