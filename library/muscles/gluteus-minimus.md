The smallest and deepest of the three glute muscles, lying directly beneath the gluteus medius. It starts on the outer ilium, between the middle and lower glute lines, and its tendon attaches to the front of the greater trochanter on the thigh bone. Its fibres fan out like those of the gluteus medius.

### What it does
- Lifts the thigh out to the side (abduction).
- Turns the thigh inward (internal rotation).
- Its front fibres can assist hip flexion.
- Helps keep the ball of the thigh bone centred in the socket and the pelvis level on one leg.

### In daily life
Shifting your weight onto one leg, turning on the spot with your toes pointing in, stepping off a kerb, and walking on a slope.

### Why it matters
It works beside the gluteus medius as a deep hip stabiliser, which can help the hip stay steady and reduce strain passed to the knee and lower back.

### Exercises that train it
Lateral walk, Hip abduction, Bulgarian split squat, Lunge, Single leg deadlift, Squat jack
