A short, thick chewing muscle deep in the cheek, below the temple. It has two heads that start on the sphenoid bone of the skull and run back to the neck of the lower jaw and the small disc inside the jaw joint. The mandibular branch of the trigeminal nerve supplies it.

### What it does
- Pulls the jaw forward (protrusion).
- Helps open the mouth by gliding the jaw joint forward.
- Moves the jaw to the opposite side when one side works alone, as in grinding food.

### In daily life
Opening wide for a big sandwich, chewing, talking, and yawning.

### Why it matters
It guides the disc and jaw joint through each bite. That supports comfortable chewing and speech.
