A small band of skeletal muscle in the female pelvic floor. It forms part of the urethral sphincter group and loops around the urethra and the front wall of the vagina, so it can squeeze both.

### What it does
- Squeezes the urethra shut, helping hold urine in
- Narrows the vaginal opening a little as it tightens
- Adds to the closing pressure of the urethra when the abdomen is under strain

### In daily life
It tightens on its own when you cough, sneeze or laugh, and you can also squeeze it on purpose to hold back urine until you reach a toilet.

### Why it matters
Along with the rest of the pelvic floor it supports bladder control. Keeping these muscles strong and able to relax can help with continence, especially after childbirth or with age.
