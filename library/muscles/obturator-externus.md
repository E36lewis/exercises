A flat, fan-shaped muscle deep in the upper inner thigh. It runs from the outer surface of the obturator membrane and the bony edge around it to a pit on the back of the thigh bone (the trochanteric fossa), passing below the hip joint.

### What it does
- Turns the thigh outward (lateral rotation).
- Helps hold the head of the thigh bone in its socket.
- Steadies the hip as you move.
- Helps bring the thigh inward.

### In daily life
Crossing a leg over the other, pivoting on one foot and turning the foot out when you get out of a car.

### Why it matters
It is one of the deep hip rotators that stabilise the hip joint while larger muscles move the leg.

### Exercises that train it
Squat, Deadlift, Single leg romanian deadlift, Clam, Standing side throw, Russian twist
