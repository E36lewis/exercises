Eleven thin muscles on each side of the ribcage, one in each gap between the ribs. Their fibres run downward and forward, in the same direction as your hands going into front pockets, and they are the outermost of the three intercostal layers.

### What it does
- Raise the ribs during breathing in, widening the chest.
- Keep the rib spaces firm so they do not suck in or bulge out as pressure changes.
- Help turn and bend the trunk a little.

### In daily life
They work every time you breathe, and more when you take a deep breath, sigh, climb stairs or work hard in a sprint.

### Why it matters
With the diaphragm, they do the work of getting air in. Good rib movement can help with posture and endurance, and stiff ribs after injury can make deep breaths feel sharp or tight. They are supplied by the intercostal nerves, one from each spinal level in the chest.
