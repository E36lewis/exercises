A deep muscle lying on the front of the spine, against the vertebral bodies. It runs from the upper chest vertebrae up to the first neck vertebra (the atlas) and has three parts: vertical, upper oblique and lower oblique. It sits behind the throat and the other neck flexors.

### What it does
- Bends the neck forward, as in a gentle chin tuck.
- Bends the neck to the side when one side works.
- Helps turn the neck slightly.
- Holds the neck curve steady while larger muscles move the head.

### In daily life
Nodding "yes", tucking your chin to look at your phone or lap, and keeping your head balanced over your shoulders while you read or work at a desk.

### Why it matters
It is one of the deep stabilisers of the neck. Training it with gentle chin tucks can help you hold an upright head position and may ease the strain placed on the larger neck muscles.
