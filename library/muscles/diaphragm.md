A broad, dome-shaped sheet of muscle that separates the chest from the abdomen. It attaches to the lower ribs, the breastbone and the lumbar spine, and its fibres meet in a central tendon.

### What it does
- Contracts and flattens to draw air into the lungs (inhalation).
- Raises pressure inside the abdomen when you brace, cough or strain.
- Works with the deep abdominal muscles and pelvic floor to support the spine.

### In daily life
It powers every breath you take, including the deep breaths before a shout or a sigh of relief. It also helps with hiccups, laughing and lifting a heavy box.

### Why it matters
It is the main breathing muscle, and slow breathing from the belly uses it well. It also forms part of the trunk's pressure system, which can help steady the spine under load. It is supplied by the phrenic nerve, which comes from the neck.
