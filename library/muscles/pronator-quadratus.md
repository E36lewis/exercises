A small, flat, square muscle on the front of the forearm, close to the wrist. It lies deepest of the front forearm muscles and links the two forearm bones, the ulna and the radius.

### What it does
- Pronates the forearm (turns the palm down)
- Helps hold the two forearm bones together near the wrist, especially when you grip or push

### In daily life
You use it when you turn a doorknob, pour from a jug, wring out a cloth or rest your palm flat on a keyboard.

### Why it matters
It starts the pronating turn and keeps the forearm bones snug, which can support the wrist when you grip, press or carry. It works with the larger pronator teres as the load rises.
