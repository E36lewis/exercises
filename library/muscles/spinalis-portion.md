The innermost of the three columns of the erector spinae. It is a thin strip beside the spinous processes, mostly in the thoracic spine, with small parts reaching the neck and the skull.

### What it does
- Extends the spine when both sides work (arches the back)
- Side-bends the spine when one side works
- Keeps the vertebrae in line as larger back muscles move the trunk

### In daily life
It works when you straighten up after tying a shoe, keep your back upright while carrying a heavy bag and hold your chest tall while you walk.

### Why it matters
Because it sits right next to the spine, it can help control small movements between vertebrae. Training it with the other spinal erectors supports posture and a strong, steady back for lifting.

### Exercises that train it
Bird dog, hyperextension, cat stretch, running, pendlay row, zercher squat
