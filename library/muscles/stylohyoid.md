A slender muscle in the upper neck. It runs from the styloid process, a thin spike of bone below the ear, down to the hyoid bone. It sits along the back of the digastric muscle, and its tendon splits around that muscle's tendon.

### What it does
- Pulls the hyoid bone up and back
- Lengthens the floor of the mouth as the hyoid moves
- Helps start the swallow by lifting the base of the tongue

### In daily life
It works each time you swallow your saliva, a sip of water or a bite of food, lifting the hyoid and tongue base up and back toward the throat.

### Why it matters
Swallowing depends on many small muscles firing in the right order. The stylohyoid is one of them, so it supports comfortable eating and drinking, but it is not a training target.
