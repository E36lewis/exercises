The shorter of the two fibularis muscles on the outer side of the lower leg, lying under the fibularis longus. It starts on the lower half of the fibula and its tendon passes behind the outer ankle bone to reach the base of the fifth metatarsal, the bone behind the little toe.

### What it does
- Turns the sole of the foot outward (eversion).
- Helps point the foot down (plantar flexion), weakly.
- Holds the outer side of the ankle steady.

### In daily life
It stops your ankle rolling inward when you walk on a trail, step off a curb at an angle or cut sideways in sport.

### Why it matters
It is one of the main muscles that guards the outer ankle. Weak or slow fibularis muscles are linked with repeated ankle sprains, so training them can help protect that joint. It is supplied by the superficial fibular nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Running, Deadlift, Lateral leap, Pistol squat, Cross hop, Single leg forward hop
