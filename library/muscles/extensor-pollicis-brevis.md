A short muscle on the thumb side of the back of the forearm, beside the abductor pollicis longus. Its tendon crosses the wrist and ends on the first bone of the thumb. Together they form the front edge of the "anatomical snuffbox", the small hollow at the base of the thumb.

### What it does
- Straightens (extends) the thumb at its base joint.
- Helps move the thumb away from the palm side and out to the side.
- Helps move the wrist toward the thumb side.

### In daily life
It opens the thumb when you let go of a jar lid, make a "C" shape to grip a large cup or hitchhike.

### Why it matters
Its tendon shares a tight tunnel at the wrist with the abductor pollicis longus. Repeated thumb movements, such as gripping a phone or lifting a baby, can irritate that tunnel (de Quervain's tenosynovitis), so strength and rest matter here. It is supplied by the posterior interosseous nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Lean back forearm, Side to side forearm
