A muscle on the little-finger side of the front of the forearm. It starts from the inner elbow and the ulna and ends on the pisiform (a small wrist bone) and the base of the fifth metacarpal. It is the only wrist flexor supplied by the ulnar nerve.

### What it does
- Bends the wrist (flexion).
- Tilts the wrist toward the little finger side (ulnar deviation).
- Acts with the extensor carpi ulnaris to hold the wrist steady.

### In daily life
It tilts the wrist when you carry a bucket, hammer, swing a racket or throw a ball with a flick.

### Why it matters
It stabilises the wrist during gripping and swinging, which can help protect it. Because the ulnar nerve runs under its heads at the elbow ("cubital tunnel"), tightness here can contribute to tingling in the little finger. It is supplied by the ulnar nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Wrist curl palm up, Farmer carry, Lean back forearm, Side to side forearm, Reverse bicep curl
