Short muscles that span the gap between the side projections (transverse processes) of neighbouring vertebrae. They are found mainly in the neck and the lower back. Each level has a front and back set, supplied by the ventral and dorsal branches of the spinal nerves.

### What it does
- Bend the spine sideways, a small amount at each level.
- Hold single vertebrae steady while larger muscles move the trunk.
- Send position information from the spine to the brain.

### In daily life
Tilting your head toward your shoulder when you hold a phone, leaning to one side to see round a corner, and small sway adjustments when you stand.

### Why it matters
They help the spine sense where it is and stay steady segment by segment. This supports the work of the deeper and larger back muscles.
