A group of small, deep back muscles on each side of the spine, one at each level, most developed in the chest (thoracic) region. Each runs from the transverse process of one vertebra to the base of the spinous process of the vertebra above (rotatores brevis) or two above (longus).

### What it does
- Rotate the vertebrae toward the opposite side
- Extend the spine slightly
- Probably sense the position of each vertebra, as they are rich in stretch receptors

### In daily life
They help when you twist to look behind you, reach across your body or turn as you walk.

### Why it matters
They are too small to be strong movers, but they give fine control of each spinal level. That supports spinal stability and posture and may help you feel where your back is.

### Exercises that train it
Single arm bent over press, front squats flag pole both arm, wide feet hamstring stretch
