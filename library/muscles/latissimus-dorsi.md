A large, flat, triangular muscle that covers the lower back and the sides of the trunk. It starts on the lower spine through the thoracolumbar fascia, the back of the pelvis and the lower ribs. It then narrows and twists to attach to the front of the upper arm bone. It is often called the lats.

### What it does
- Pulls the raised arm down and back (shoulder extension).
- Brings the arm toward the body (adduction).
- Turns the arm inward.
- Helps with deep breathing and coughing.

### In daily life
Pulling a heavy door open, climbing a ladder, swimming a stroke, starting a lawnmower and carrying a bag at your side.

### Why it matters
It is the main muscle for pulling the arm down toward the body. Strong lats can help balance the chest muscles and support good shoulder position.

### Exercises that train it
Pull up, Chin up, Row, Inverted row, Pendlay row, Pull over
