Short paired muscles that join the pointed bumps (spinous processes) on the backs of neighbouring vertebrae. They are best developed in the neck and the lower back, and are often absent or small in the mid-back. They sit in a deep layer, close to the spine.

### What it does
- Help extend the spine, a small amount at each level.
- Make fine adjustments to the position of single vertebrae.
- Send position information to the brain, because they contain many sensing fibres.

### In daily life
Small corrections of posture as you tip your head up to look at a high shelf or lean back in a chair.

### Why it matters
Their main value may be sensory: they help the brain track the spine's position. That supports control and coordination of the larger back muscles.

### Exercises that train it
Hyperextension, Reverse hyperextension, Kettlebell swing, Squat, Twisting spine, Turkish get up
