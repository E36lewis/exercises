A small, flat, square muscle at the back of the hip, beneath the gluteus maximus. It runs from the sit bone (ischial tuberosity) straight across to the back of the upper thigh bone.

### What it does
- Externally rotates the hip (turns the thigh and foot outward)
- Helps adduct the thigh (bring it toward the midline)
- Helps keep the ball of the thigh bone seated in its socket

### In daily life
It helps when you turn your foot outward, cross one leg over the other, sit in a cross-legged position or pivot on one foot.

### Why it matters
It is one of the deep hip rotators. Together with its neighbours it supports hip stability and can help control the thigh during squatting, stepping and changes of direction.

### Exercises that train it
Hip thrust, hip abduction
