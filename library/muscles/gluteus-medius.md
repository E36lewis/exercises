A thick, fan-shaped muscle on the outer side of the hip. It starts on the outer surface of the ilium (the wing of the pelvis) and narrows to a tendon on the greater trochanter of the thigh bone. Its back part lies under the gluteus maximus, and the front part sits just under the skin and fascia.

### What it does
- Lifts the thigh out to the side (abduction).
- Its front fibres turn the thigh inward; its back fibres turn it outward.
- Holds the pelvis level when one foot is off the ground.

### In daily life
Walking without your hips dipping on each step, balancing on one leg to pull on a sock, stepping sideways through a crowd, and climbing stairs.

### Why it matters
This is the hip's main side stabiliser. Keeping it strong can help the knee track over the foot and support a steady, even stride.

### Exercises that train it
Lateral walk, Hip abduction, Clam, Bulgarian split squat, Single leg deadlift, Lateral single leg hop
