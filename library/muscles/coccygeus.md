A thin triangular muscle at the back of the pelvic floor. It runs from the ischial spine (a bony point on the sit bone) to the side of the coccyx (tailbone) and the lowest part of the sacrum. It lies on top of a ligament of the same size.

### What it does
- Supports the back part of the pelvic floor.
- Pulls the coccyx forward after it has been pushed back, as in childbirth or a bowel movement.
- Helps hold pelvic organs in place.

### In daily life
It works in the background whenever you cough, strain or lift something heavy, resisting the rise in pressure inside the abdomen.

### Why it matters
It completes the back of the pelvic floor and so helps the floor carry the weight of the organs above it. It is supplied by branches of the fourth and fifth sacral nerves.
