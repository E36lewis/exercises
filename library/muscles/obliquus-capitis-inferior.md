A small suboccipital muscle at the top of the neck. It runs from the spine of the second neck vertebra (the axis) to the side of the first (the atlas). It is the only suboccipital muscle that does not attach to the skull.

### What it does
- Turns the atlas, and with it the head, to the same side.
- Helps fine-tune head position.
- Holds the top two vertebrae steady when other muscles work.
- Sends position information about the head to the brain.

### In daily life
Looking over your shoulder when you reverse the car or check traffic, and small head turns while you talk.

### Why it matters
It lets you turn your head smoothly at the top of the neck. The suboccipitals are rich in position sensors, so they help with head balance and eye-head coordination.

### Exercises that train it
Neck banded hold, Neck stretch
