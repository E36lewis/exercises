A muscle deep in the middle of the sole, with two heads. The oblique head runs diagonally from the midfoot bones, and the transverse head runs across the ball of the foot. Both join at the base of the big toe.

### What it does
- Pulls the big toe toward the second toe (adduction).
- Helps bend the big toe at its base.
- Draws the front of the foot together, supporting the arch that runs across the ball of the foot (the transverse arch).

### In daily life
It lets your big toe press into the ground when you push off to take a step, and it helps you grip with your toes when you stand on a narrow ledge or a wobbly surface.

### Why it matters
It pulls the forefoot together, which can help the ball of the foot stay stable under load. It is supplied by the deep branch of the lateral plantar nerve.
