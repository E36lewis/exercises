A slender muscle on the back of the forearm, next to the extensor digitorum. Its tendon crosses the wrist and joins the tendon of the extensor digitorum on the little finger, so it can straighten that finger by itself.

### What it does
- Straightens (extends) the little finger at the knuckle.
- Helps extend the wrist when the fingers are working together.
- Lets the little finger lift on its own, away from the ring finger.

### In daily life
It raises your little finger when you reach for the Shift key, spread your hand flat on a table or hold a teacup with the little finger out.

### Why it matters
This muscle is one reason the little finger can move on its own. It works against the flexors that close the hand, which can help keep the back of the forearm and wrist balanced. It is supplied by the posterior interosseous nerve, the deep branch of the radial nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Finger extensions, Wrist curl palm down, Lean back forearm, Push up fingers, Side to side forearm
