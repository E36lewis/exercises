A pear-shaped muscle deep in the buttock. It runs from the front of the sacrum, through the greater sciatic notch, to the top of the thigh bone. The sciatic nerve lies right next to it, and in some people passes through it.

### What it does
- Turns the thigh outward when the hip is straight.
- Moves the bent thigh away from the midline (abduction).
- Steadies the hip joint.
- Helps hold the head of the thigh bone in its socket.

### In daily life
Turning your foot out, sitting cross-legged and stepping to the side.

### Why it matters
Because the sciatic nerve is so close, a tight piriformis is often blamed for buttock and leg discomfort. Hip mobility and stretching can help, and persistent leg pain needs a professional's advice.

### Exercises that train it
Hip abduction, Lateral walk, Clam, Thigh squeezes, Standing side throw, Russian twist
