A small, narrow muscle deep on the back of the forearm, beneath the extensor digitorum. Its tendon runs beside the index-finger tendon of the extensor digitorum and gives the index finger an extra extensor of its own.

### What it does
- Straightens (extends) the index finger at the knuckle.
- Lets the index finger lift on its own while the other fingers stay curled.
- Helps extend the wrist.

### In daily life
It is the muscle you use to point at something while the rest of your hand holds a fist, tap a screen with one finger or hold a pen with the index finger lifted.

### Why it matters
The index finger is the most independent finger, and this muscle is a large part of why. Its tendon is also sometimes used by surgeons as a donor tendon, because the finger still extends with the extensor digitorum. It is supplied by the posterior interosseous nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Finger extensions, Lean back forearm, Side to side forearm, Push up fingers, Wrist curl palm down
