The largest and strongest rotator cuff muscle. It fills the front surface of the shoulder blade, lying between the scapula and the ribs, and its tendon crosses the front of the joint to the lesser tubercle of the upper arm bone.

### What it does
- Rotates the arm inward (internal rotation)
- Holds the head of the humerus against the socket, especially at the front
- Helps lower the raised arm and bring it toward the body

### In daily life
It works when you reach behind your back to tuck in a shirt, fasten a bra or belt, or throw a ball and swing the arm across the body.

### Why it matters
Because it covers the front of the joint, it helps keep the shoulder from sliding forward. A strong subscapularis can support shoulder stability in pressing, throwing and overhead lifting.

### Exercises that train it
Rotatory cuff internal rotation, windmill, clean, overhead press, seated shoulder press
