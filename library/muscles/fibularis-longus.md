The longer, more superficial muscle on the outer side of the lower leg. Its tendon passes behind the outer ankle bone, turns under the foot and crosses the sole to the inner side near the base of the big toe.

### What it does
- Turns the sole of the foot outward (eversion).
- Helps point the foot down (plantar flexion).
- Supports the arches of the foot and presses the base of the big toe into the ground.

### In daily life
It works when you balance on one leg, push off the big toe to run or hop, or walk on a sloping path.

### Why it matters
Like a stirrup, its tendon helps hold the foot's arches and the ankle together, which can protect against sprains and help with balance. It is supplied by the superficial fibular nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Running, Pistol squat, Lateral leap, Cross hop, Single leg lateral hop, Foot arch roll
