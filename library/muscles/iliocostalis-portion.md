The outermost of the three columns of the erector spinae, the long muscle strips that run beside the spine. It starts low, on the sacrum and the back of the pelvis, and each part attaches to the ribs and to the lower neck vertebrae above. Its lumbar, thoracic and cervical parts are named for the region they cover.

### What it does
- Extends the spine, bending it backward.
- Bends the trunk to the same side when one side works alone.
- Pulls the ribs down and holds the trunk upright against gravity.

### In daily life
Standing tall in a queue, carrying a shopping bag on one side, rising from a forward bend, and lifting a box off the floor.

### Why it matters
These muscles hold the spine upright for most of the day. Strong back extensors can help the spine tolerate lifting and long periods of standing.

### Exercises that train it
Back extension, Hyperextension, Reverse hyperextension, Bird dog, Romanian deadlift, Cat stretch
