A small, flat, triangular muscle at the back of the knee. It runs from the outer end of the thigh bone (lateral femoral condyle) down and inward to the back of the shin bone, just below the knee.

### What it does
- Turns the shin inward on the thigh, or the thigh outward when the foot is planted
- Unlocks the knee at the start of bending from a straight position
- Resists the shin bone sliding backward and twisting outward

### In daily life
It works when you bend your knees after standing still, walk downhill or pivot on a planted foot, such as turning to grab something off a shelf.

### Why it matters
It is a small knee stabiliser, so it can help you control the joint when you squat, land from a jump or change direction.

### Exercises that train it
Step up, squat, pistol squat, running, nordic curl
