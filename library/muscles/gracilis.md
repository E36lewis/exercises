A long, thin, strap-like muscle down the inner thigh. It starts on the pubic bone and runs straight down to the inner side of the shin bone just below the knee, where its tendon joins those of the sartorius and semitendinosus (the "goose foot"). Of the inner thigh adductors, it is the only one that crosses the knee.

### What it does
- Brings the thigh toward the midline (hip adduction).
- Helps bend the knee.
- Turns the bent lower leg slightly inward.
- Helps steady the inner side of the knee.

### In daily life
Squeezing your knees together on a bicycle or horse, crossing your legs, stepping sideways, and swinging a leg across your body.

### Why it matters
It links the groin to the inner knee, so it takes part in side-to-side moves and changes of direction. Keeping the adductors strong and flexible can help with cutting sports.

### Exercises that train it
Hip adduction, Butterfly, Low squat, Goblet squat, Squat jack, Switch lunge
