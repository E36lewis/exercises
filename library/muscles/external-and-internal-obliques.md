Two layers of muscle on each side of the abdomen. The external oblique is the outer one, with fibres that run like your hands going into front pockets. The internal oblique lies under it, with fibres running at a right angle to them.

### What it does
- Bend the trunk sideways (lateral flexion) when one side works.
- Rotate the trunk: an external oblique works with the internal oblique on the opposite side.
- Bend the trunk forward and flatten the belly when both sides work.
- Brace the abdomen and help force air out when you cough or breathe out hard.

### In daily life
They help you twist to look behind you, reach across to buckle a seat belt, swing a golf club or bat, and carry a bag on one side without leaning over.

### Why it matters
The obliques resist unwanted twisting as well as create it, which supports the spine in lifting and in sports with throwing or swinging. They are supplied by the lower intercostal nerves, along with the subcostal and branches from the lower back (L1).

### Exercises that train it
Oblique crunches, Russian twist, Wood choppers, Banded core twist, Kneeling side throw, Lunge pass through
