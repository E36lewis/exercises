A flat, fan-shaped muscle that lines the inside of the hip bone, in the hollow called the iliac fossa. It joins the tendon of the psoas major and the two share an attachment at the lesser trochanter, high on the inner thigh bone. The pair is called the iliopsoas, the body's strongest hip flexor.

### What it does
- Bends the hip, lifting the thigh toward the trunk.
- Tips the pelvis forward when the thigh is held still.
- Helps steady the hip joint during walking.

### In daily life
Lifting your foot onto a step, kicking a ball, swinging your leg into a car, and driving the knee up in a run.

### Why it matters
It sets the length of your stride and supports pelvic position. Long hours of sitting can leave hip flexors short and tight, and gentle strengthening and mobility work can help.

### Exercises that train it
Hanging leg raise, Running, Running in place, Swing kick, Reverse lunge, Pistol squat
