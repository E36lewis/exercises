A short, narrow muscle in the floor of the mouth, lying just above the mylohyoid. It starts on the inside of the chin (the genial tubercle) and runs back to the hyoid bone, the small U-shaped bone under the tongue. It is one of the suprahyoid muscles, the group that sits between the jaw and the hyoid.

### What it does
- Pulls the hyoid bone up and forward, which also draws the voice box forward.
- Helps lower the jaw (open the mouth) when the hyoid is held in place by the muscles below it.
- Widens the space behind the tongue during swallowing.

### In daily life
Swallowing food or a sip of water, opening your jaw to take a big bite, yawning, and singing a long open note.

### Why it matters
The hyoid acts like a moving anchor for the tongue and throat. This muscle can help swallowing stay smooth and the airway stay open.
