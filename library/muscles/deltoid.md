The thick, rounded cap of the shoulder, with front, side and rear parts. It starts from the collarbone, the acromion and the spine of the shoulder blade, and attaches halfway down the outer upper arm.

### What it does
- The front part lifts the arm forward (flexion).
- The side part lifts it out to the side (abduction).
- The rear part pulls it backward (extension).

### In daily life
It raises your arm to put a suitcase on a rack, to reach a cup on a high cupboard shelf, or to wave to a friend.

### Why it matters
The deltoid gives the shoulder its round shape and supplies much of the strength in overhead and lifting movements. It also helps hold the arm bone in its socket when you carry a heavy bag. It is supplied by the axillary nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Overhead press, Arnold press, Lateral raise, Front arm raise, Reverse flye, Upright row
