A small, narrow muscle deep in the buttock, running along the upper edge of the obturator internus tendon. It starts on the ischial spine, a bony point at the back of the pelvis, and attaches to the greater trochanter on the outer upper thigh bone. Together with the gemellus inferior it forms a pair that sandwiches the obturator internus tendon.

### What it does
- Turns the thigh outward (external rotation) at the hip.
- Helps pull the thigh bone into its socket for stability.
- Can help move the thigh sideways when the hip is bent.

### In daily life
Planting a foot and rotating your body over it, turning outward as you walk around a corner, and steadying the hip when you step sideways.

### Why it matters
The deep rotators give the hip fine control that the big glute muscles cannot. This supports balanced, steady footing.

### Exercises that train it
Lateral walk, Step up, Running, Cross hop hold, Clam, Standing side throw
