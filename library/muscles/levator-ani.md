A broad, bowl-shaped sheet of muscle that forms most of the pelvic floor. It has three parts: the puborectalis, pubococcygeus and iliococcygeus. They run from the pubic bone and the side wall of the pelvis back to the tailbone and around the rectum, and they leave openings for the urethra, vagina (in women) and anal canal.

### What it does
- Holds up the pelvic organs.
- Closes the openings of the bladder and bowel against pressure from above.
- Lifts the pelvic floor and tightens it when you brace.
- Relaxes to allow passing urine, stool and, in childbirth, the baby.

### In daily life
Staying dry when you cough, laugh, jump or lift, and relaxing fully when you use the toilet.

### Why it matters
A well-working pelvic floor supports continence and the base of the core. Pelvic floor training can help after childbirth or surgery, and with age.
