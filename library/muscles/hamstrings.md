The group of three muscles at the back of the thigh: biceps femoris, semitendinosus and semimembranosus. Most of them start on the sit bone and cross both the hip and the knee, ending on the lower leg bones just below the knee. The short head of the biceps femoris starts on the thigh bone instead and crosses only the knee.

### What it does
- Bend the knee.
- Extend the hip, drawing the thigh backward.
- Turn the bent lower leg: the biceps femoris outward, the two inner hamstrings inward.
- Slow the lower leg as it swings forward in running and kicking.

### In daily life
Walking, running, bending your knee to put on a shoe, climbing a hill, and hinging forward to pick something up.

### Why it matters
Strong hamstrings help the knee and pelvis stay stable and are often worked to protect against strains in sprinting. Training them through a full range of movement may help.

### Exercises that train it
Romanian deadlift, Deadlift, Nordic curl, Hamstring curls, Single leg deadlift, Kettlebell swing
