A long muscle deep in the lower back and pelvis. It starts on the sides of the lower spine (the last thoracic and the lumbar vertebrae), passes over the front of the pelvis and joins the iliacus to attach to the top of the thigh bone (femur). Together they are called the iliopsoas, the main hip flexor.

### What it does
- Flexes the hip, lifting the thigh toward the trunk
- Flexes and side-bends the lower spine when the leg is fixed
- Helps hold the lower spine steady when you stand and walk

### In daily life
It swings the leg forward as you walk, lifts your knee on a stair, and helps you sit up from lying down or step into a car.

### Why it matters
It links the spine to the leg, so a tight or weak psoas can affect lower back comfort and posture. Balancing it with the glutes and abdominals supports a healthy hip and spine.

### Exercises that train it
High knees, lying floor leg raise, band plank march, hanging leg raise, switch lunge, lunge
