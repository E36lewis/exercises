The longest muscle in the body, a thin strap that runs diagonally down the thigh. It starts at the front of the pelvis (anterior superior iliac spine) and ends on the inner upper shin bone. Its name comes from the Latin for tailor, after the cross-legged sitting position.

### What it does
- Flexes the hip
- Abducts and externally rotates the thigh
- Flexes the knee and turns the shin inward

### In daily life
It works when you sit cross-legged, put on a sock with your foot resting on your other knee or step sideways over an obstacle.

### Why it matters
It acts at both the hip and the knee, and its tendon on the inner knee helps keep that joint steady. It can help with the footwork in side lunges and kicks.

### Exercises that train it
Lateral walk, goblet squat, squat, swing kick, side lunge, high knees
