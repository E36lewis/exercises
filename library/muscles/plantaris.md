A thin, slender muscle at the back of the knee and calf. Its small belly sits behind the knee, and a very long, thin tendon runs down beside the Achilles tendon to the heel. A fair number of people are born without one.

### What it does
- Assists plantar flexion (pointing the foot down)
- Gives a little help in bending the knee
- Works alongside the gastrocnemius and soleus, which do most of the work

### In daily life
It adds a small amount of help when you rise onto your toes, climb stairs or push off to jump, though you would not notice it was missing.

### Why it matters
It is a minor helper, so training the whole calf is what counts. Because it is often absent, its tendon is sometimes a spare for surgeons to use in other repairs.

### Exercises that train it
Jump squat, calf hop, reach jump, frog leap, squat jack, lunge
