A short, flat muscle at the base of the thumb on the palm side. It is the outermost of the thenar muscles, which form the ball of the thumb, and it runs from the wrist to the first bone of the thumb.

### What it does
- Moves the thumb forward and away from the palm (palmar abduction).
- Helps bring the thumb across to meet the fingers (opposition).

### In daily life
It lifts the thumb up and out so it is ready to take hold of something, such as a coin lying flat on a table, a pen, or the edge of a phone as you pick it up.

### Why it matters
Placing the thumb in front of the palm is what lets you pinch and grasp. Without this movement, much of the hand's skill is lost. It is supplied by the recurrent branch of the median nerve, the nerve that passes through the carpal tunnel.
