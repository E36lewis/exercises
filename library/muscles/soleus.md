A broad, flat calf muscle that lies under the gastrocnemius. It runs from the back of the shin and the top of the fibula to the heel through the Achilles tendon. It crosses only the ankle, not the knee.

### What it does
- Plantar flexes the ankle (points the foot down)
- Keeps working when the knee is bent, when the gastrocnemius is slack
- Helps keep the lower leg steady over the foot when standing

### In daily life
It works when you stand for long periods, walk, climb stairs or rise onto your toes, and it keeps you upright while you stand at a counter.

### Why it matters
It is a postural muscle that supports steady standing and walking. Its action also helps the veins of the leg return blood to the heart. Seated calf raises, with the knee bent, train it more than straight-leg ones.

### Exercises that train it
Calf raise, calf wall, running, jump squat, lunge, hack squat
