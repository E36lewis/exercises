A small, flat muscle on the top of the foot. It starts on the heel bone (calcaneus) and its tendons join those of the extensor digitorum longus on the way to the toes. The part that goes to the big toe is often counted as a separate muscle, the extensor hallucis brevis.

### What it does
- Extends (lifts) the second, third and fourth toes at their base joints.
- Adds to the pull of the longer toe extensor on the same toes.

### In daily life
It helps lift your toes as your foot swings forward, so they clear the ground. You can see it as a soft bulge on the outer top of the foot just in front of the ankle.

### Why it matters
It is a small helper, but it can fill in for the longer toe extensor if the foot is stiff or sore. Its bulk is sometimes mistaken for a bruise or swelling after an ankle sprain. It is supplied by the deep fibular nerve.
