A muscle on the back of the forearm, shorter than its neighbour the extensor carpi radialis longus. It starts at the common extensor tendon on the outer elbow and attaches to the base of the third metacarpal, behind the middle finger.

### What it does
- Extends the wrist (bends the hand back).
- Tilts the wrist toward the thumb side (radial deviation).
- Holds the wrist firm as you grip.

### In daily life
It cocks your wrist back when you lift a frying pan, hit a tennis forehand or steady your hand on a mouse.

### Why it matters
It keeps the wrist in a good position for gripping, and it is the tendon most often involved in tennis elbow. It is supplied by the posterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the radial nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Wrist curl palm down, Finger extensions, Lean back forearm, Side to side forearm, Push up fingers, Hammer curl
