A thin muscle along the outer edge of the sole. It runs from the heel bone to the outside of the base of the little toe, and it is the foot's counterpart of the muscle that spreads the little finger.

### What it does
- Moves the little toe away from the other toes (abduction).
- Helps bend the little toe at its base.
- Helps support the outer edge of the foot as it meets the ground.

### In daily life
It works as you walk across a sloping lawn or a pebbled path, when the outer edge of the foot has to adjust to the surface. It also helps when you run and the outer foot takes your weight.

### Why it matters
Its position along the outer border adds side-to-side stability to the foot, which can help balance on uneven ground. It is supplied by the lateral plantar nerve, a branch of the tibial nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Running, Toe flexor
