The inner thigh muscles: adductor longus, adductor brevis and the large adductor magnus, often grouped with the pectineus and gracilis. Most run from the pubic and sit bones of the pelvis to the back of the thigh bone.

### What it does
- Pulls the thigh toward the midline of the body (hip adduction).
- Helps flex the hip, mainly the upper fibres.
- The rear part of adductor magnus helps extend the hip, working with the hamstrings.

### In daily life
They squeeze your knees together on a horse or a motorbike, hold your leg steady when you change direction, and pull your foot back under you after a step to the side.

### Why it matters
Strong, flexible adductors can help keep the pelvis steady and share the load in the groin, which can reduce the chance of strains in running and kicking sports. Most are supplied by the obturator nerve. Part of adductor magnus is supplied by the sciatic nerve.

### Exercises that train it
Hip adduction, Thigh squeezes, Sumo deadlift, Goblet squat, Side lunge, Kettlebell swing
