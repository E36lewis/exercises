A small muscle on the outer side of the upper thigh. It starts at the front of the pelvis (the anterior superior iliac spine) and joins the iliotibial band, a thick strip of fascia that runs down the outside of the thigh to the shin.

### What it does
- Flexes the hip (lifts the thigh forward)
- Abducts the hip (moves the thigh out to the side)
- Rotates the thigh inward
- Tightens the iliotibial band to help steady the knee

### In daily life
It works when you step sideways out of a car, swing your leg forward in a stride and balance on one leg to put on a sock.

### Why it matters
Through the iliotibial band it links the hip to the knee, so it supports pelvis control while you walk and run. Tightness can cause pulling on the outside of the hip or knee, so it benefits from balance with the glutes.

### Exercises that train it
Lateral walk, hip abduction, squat jack, high knees, goblet squat, circle run
