A slender muscle on the thumb side of the forearm, deep to the surface. It starts on the back of the radius, the ulna and the membrane between them, and its tendon crosses the wrist to attach at the base of the thumb's first metacarpal bone.

### What it does
- Moves the thumb out to the side, away from the palm (abduction).
- Helps extend the thumb at its base joint.
- Helps tilt the wrist toward the thumb side (radial deviation).

### In daily life
It works when you open your thumb to grip a mug handle, hold a racket or a hammer, or reach out your thumb to swipe a phone screen.

### Why it matters
Its tendon runs beside that of the short thumb extensor along the thumb side of the wrist, an area that often feels strain from repeated gripping or texting. It is supplied by the posterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the radial nerve.
