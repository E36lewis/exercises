A flat muscle on the inner wall of the pelvis. It starts around the obturator membrane, its tendon turns around the sit bone (the lesser sciatic notch) and passes out through the buttock to attach to the top of the thigh bone.

### What it does
- Turns the thigh outward when the hip is straight.
- Moves the bent thigh away from the midline (abduction).
- Helps hold the thigh bone in its socket.
- Supports the pelvic floor.

### In daily life
Pivoting on a planted foot, sitting with your legs crossed, and stepping sideways out of a tight space.

### Why it matters
It is a deep hip rotator that can help the hip stay stable as you shift your weight. Side-stepping and outward rotation drills with a band are common ways to train it.

### Exercises that train it
Clam, Hip abduction, Lateral walk, Single leg romanian deadlift, Standing side throw
